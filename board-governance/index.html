Issue history

How the board governs itself: conduct, conflicts, hiring and openness

Who holds Hanover’s school trustees to account between elections? How does the board discipline its own members, what does it decide in public and what behind closed doors, who hires the people who run the schools, and why did the province send an adviser in 2024?

Board meetings Jul 2013 – Sept 2026 · 67 decisions in the minutes · Last updated Nov 14, 2026

In short

  • Two trustees publicly sanctioned in 13 years. In 2017 the board voted 4–3 to censure Trustee Brad Unger for breaching its code of conduct. In 2025 it voted 6–0 to suspend Trustee Danielle Funk for six weeks without pay. The minutes do not say what either trustee did. Funk appealed; the Carillon reports that an adjudicator found she had broken one of the rules the board cited.
  • Much of the board’s work happens in closed meetings. Every year the board adopts the minutes of 21 to 27 closed (in-camera) Committee of the Whole meetings, about two for every public meeting. Those minutes are not published. In January 2025 Funk’s motion for an outside review of the board’s governance and openness was defeated 1–6 in a recorded vote.
  • 2024 brought the board’s closest votes on how it runs itself. Trustees voted 5–4 to hire music and gym teachers “the same way the vice principals are hired”, and 5–3 for a conflict-of-interest policy covering “real, potential or perceived” conflicts, after a debate over those words. A new hiring procedure passed 6–1 in 2025; its text is not in the minutes.
  • The province stepped in, and stepped back. The board fought Bill 64, which would have abolished elected boards; it was withdrawn in October 2021. After a parents’ group complained in April 2024, the minister appointed retired superintendent Kelly Barkman as an adviser to the board. A records request by the Carillon found no written reports from him, and he left in the winter of 2025–26.
  • A new board and a new superintendent. The division will have its third superintendent since Randy Dueck retired in 2020 when Kevin Clace starts by Jan 1, 2027. In October 2026 every ward had a contest, for the first time in the four elections since 2014, and six of the nine trustees elected are new.

Background: the rules

What the law requires

Manitoba’s Public Schools Act says every school board “shall hold its meetings openly”, but committee meetings “may be held in camera”, meaning in private. Hanover does much of its discussing as a Committee of the Whole: all nine trustees meeting as a committee, with the public excluded. Decisions that need a vote are then brought to the public meeting.

Since a 2012 amendment, every board must have a code of conduct for trustees. If the board finds a trustee broke it, the Act allows three sanctions: censure (a formal reprimand), barring the trustee from all or part of a meeting, or suspension for up to three months. A sanction may be debated in private but must be voted on in public, and barring or suspending a trustee needs the votes of two-thirds of all the board’s members, which in Hanover means six. A barred or suspended trustee can appeal to an adjudicator appointed by the minister. Hanover’s code adds that the board “has no power to declare the Trustee’s seat vacant” as a sanction.

The Act also sets attendance rules. A trustee must be physically present at a regular meeting at least once every three months, and the board must declare a seat vacant if a trustee misses three consecutive regular meetings without the board’s authorization “by resolution recorded in the minutes.”

Hanover’s own policies

  • Policy BF, Board Member Code of Conduct (last revised Jun 2, 2026). Section A.2 tells trustees to “Support the legitimacy and authority of Board decisions, irrespective of the trustee’s personal position on the issue.” Trustees must also treat colleagues, staff and the public respectfully, and avoid media or social-media comments “that would discredit, undermine or compromise the integrity of the Board” (A.6). An individual trustee cannot speak for the board “except to repeat explicitly stated Board decisions.” It also covers “relationship bias” involving family and friends, and how complaints are investigated.
  • Policy BC, Board Member Conflict of Interest (2024). A conflict “can be real, potential or perceived”, and trustees must step out of any matter where they are or may be perceived to be in conflict. Policy GP sets similar rules for employees.
  • Policy BD, Board Operations and Procedural By-Laws (2025). The chair is “the official spokesperson of the Board”. In-camera meetings are for negotiations, personnel, litigation, student files, land, security and discipline, and “Divisional planning”. Any trustee can ask for a recorded vote, which puts each name in the minutes; otherwise the minutes give only the count.
  • Policies BB, BBA and BBB set the superintendent’s duties, how one is recruited and how the board assesses the superintendent. The superintendent, hired by the board, runs the division day to day. Policy BH (2019) applies Manitoba’s whistleblower law to the division.

Policies and by-laws are passed in readings: a first reading, then second and third (final) readings, normally at a later meeting (the board calls this the “standard one meeting delay”).

2013–2018: Writing the rules, and a first censure

Four wards and a busy 2014 election

In November 2013 the board passed By-Law 7/2013 on ward boundaries. The Carillon reported that it meant the division’s six wards became four: West (Niverville, Bothwell, Kleefeld, Crystal Springs), North (Landmark, Blumenort, Mitchell), Steinbach with four seats, and South (Grunthal). In the 2014 election 11 candidates ran for the four Steinbach seats, and the two North seats were filled by acclamation (the only candidates were declared elected without a vote). Steinbach voters re-elected Ron Falk and Cyndy Friesen and added newcomers Brad Unger and Sarah Dyck. Five of the nine trustees were new. Ron Falk, first elected in 1992, chaired the board from 2014 to 2019, as he had from 2002 to 2008, according to the division.

The code of conduct is revised, 2015

Hanover has had a trustee code of conduct (Policy BF) since 1996. In 2015 the board revised its governance policies: it folded its old conflict-of-interest policy into the code of conduct, renewed its procedural by-laws (Policy BD), and on Oct 6 gave final reading to a revised Policy BF, the Trustee Code of Conduct, all unanimously. An appendix adopted the same day set out how a breach would be handled: first a private conversation, then the chair as conciliator, then the full board in Committee of the Whole, where a majority could find that a breach had occurred, with an independent investigator as an option.

The censure of Trustee Brad Unger, October 2017

At the end of the Oct 3, 2017 meeting, under “Additional Agenda items”, the minutes record that “Trustee Brad Unger exited the meeting at this time.” Then, under the heading “Trustee Matter”, Trustee Cyndy Friesen moved and Trustee Shannon Friesen seconded:

“to censure Trustee Brad Unger, under the authority of the Public Schools’ Act, Section 35.2(1) for a breach of the Hanover School Division Trustee Code of Conduct (Policy BF).”

Board minutes, chair Ron Falk presiding · Oct 3, 2017, carried 4–3

Seven trustees voted; Ruby Wiens sent regrets and Unger had left the room. No recorded vote was requested, so the minutes do not show who voted which way. A censure is a formal statement of disapproval; it does not remove a trustee or cut pay.

Outside the minutes

Steinbach Online reported that Unger was asked to leave during the discussion and vote of his censure, that he remained a trustee, and that both he and the division chose not to comment. The Carillon covered the censure in its Oct 5, 2017 print issue; that story is not online. A year later, during the 2018 campaign, the board’s correspondence list included a Carillon article headlined “Falk tight-lipped on trustee censure”, also not online. We found no public source that says what the breach was. Unger was re-elected in 2018 and 2022 and chaired the board in 2023–24.

The 2018 election

Three trustees were elected by acclamation: Lynn Barkman and Sue Doerksen in the North ward and newcomer Jonathon Driedger in Grunthal. Ruby Wiens, Cyndy Friesen and Sarah Dyck left the board, and Carisa Klassen and Danielle Funk joined it along with Driedger. The division’s senior election official later put turnout at 22.9%.

2019–2022: The province reviews school boards

The K–12 Commission and new governance policies

In 2019 Manitoba’s Commission on Kindergarten to Grade 12 Education asked for submissions. The board expected it to consider merging divisions and appointing, rather than electing, boards. The board’s written submission, which the division later linked from its Bill 64 letter, said the trustees “strongly oppose any mandated reduction of school divisions in Manitoba, or the elimination of locally elected school boards.” It also asked the province to encourage more sharing between divisions. Board chair Ron Falk sent the same message to parents by e-mail and told the Carillon it was “a public awareness thing” because few parents knew about the commission.

In 2019 the board also renewed its policies on the board itself (BA), conflict of interest (BC) and its by-laws (BD), adopted a public interest disclosure (whistleblower) policy, and gave final reading to a revised Board Member Code of Conduct. Every vote was unanimous. Rick Peters chaired the board for 2019–20; Falk returned as chair from 2020 to 2023.

An interim superintendent and a live-streamed meeting, 2020

When Superintendent Randy Dueck retired in 2020, the board named Assistant Superintendent Shelley Amos interim Superintendent/CEO. Board chair Rick Peters told the Carillon the board “had no choice”: the province had it pull its job ad, and a spokesperson for Education Minister Kelvin Goertzen said the division was advised “to limit the employment contract to interim or term status” until the commission reported.

With the boardroom closed by the pandemic, the division broadcast the Jun 2, 2020 meeting live on YouTube, and in December 2020 trustees met virtually. HSD does not post recordings of its meetings today.

That year the board also cleared out its policy manual. In four rounds from June 2020 to February 2021 it rescinded 98 “parked” policies that were outdated, replaced or covered elsewhere, and it adopted its first employee conflict-of-interest policy (GP).

Bill 64 would have ended elected boards

In March 2021 the provincial government released Bill 64, the Education Modernization Act. It would have dissolved every school board and ended every trustee’s term, putting a central, appointed education authority in their place. On Apr 21, 2021 all nine trustees signed a letter to the community:

“As we have stated in the past, the HSD Board of Trustees strongly opposes eliminating locally elected school boards. Our view on this matter remains unchanged. School boards are grassroots democratic institutions.”

HSD Board of Trustees, response to Bill 64 · Apr 21, 2021

The letter urged residents to register to speak to the bill or contact their MLA. Falk asked the Carillon, “With the local voice gone, who’s going to be speaking to the issues surrounding the schools or the community?” J.L., a retired Steinbach teacher who had sat on the commission, wrote to the paper that abolishing elected boards was “a course of action not recommended by the Commission”, which had proposed consolidating boards into as many as eight regional ones. The legislature’s bill-status record shows Bill 64 “Withdrawn Oct. 6, 2021”. In February 2022 the board made Amos permanent superintendent; Falk said the wait was “due to uncertainty surrounding Bill 64.”

Leave, an outside-organization rule, and the 2022 election

In March 2021 the board passed a public motion granting Trustee Shannon Friesen an unpaid leave of absence until she withdrew the request or the 2022 election, 8–0. In late 2022 it added an “External Organization Clause” to the code of conduct: a trustee who belongs to an organization may not appear with its delegation and must step out of any vote on that organization’s request.

In 2022 Rick Peters, Shannon Friesen, Carisa Klassen and Jonathon Driedger were not on the ballot, and three trustees were again acclaimed: Jeff Friesen and Dallas Wiebe in the West ward and Cheryl Froese in Grunthal. In the North ward incumbent Sue Doerksen finished third behind Charmaine Toews and Shayne Barkman. Five of the nine trustees were new, and turnout fell to 16.2%.

2023–2024: Split votes on hiring and conflicts, and a provincial adviser

A new chair, then a new superintendent

In September 2023 Trustee Brad Unger defeated Ron Falk for the chair. Since then the chair has changed every year. In February 2024 Superintendent Amos resigned to become Superintendent of Education/CEO at Pembina Trails in Winnipeg. The board ran an executive search through a consulting firm and in April named Mitchell Elementary principal Joe Thiessen superintendent from Aug 1, 2024. Superintendent appointments are made in closed session; the public minutes contain no motion appointing any superintendent.

Trustees and teacher hiring

In March 2024 Trustee Shayne Barkman gave notice of three motions, announcing them so they could be debated and voted on at the next meeting. On Apr 2 the board voted on them. The first, moved by Barkman and seconded by Trustee Dallas Wiebe, was “that all music and gym teachers be hired the same way the vice principals are hired”, that is, as the Carillon explained, by school trustees and the superintendent rather than by an assistant superintendent working with the principal. It carried 5–4. According to Steinbach Online, Shayne Barkman, Lynn Barkman, Cheryl Froese, Jeff Friesen and Dallas Wiebe voted for it, and Ron Falk, Danielle Funk, Charmaine Toews and Brad Unger against. The second, to let trustees see the full list of applicants for principal and vice-principal jobs, was defeated 4–5. (The third, on Grade 7 health classes, is covered in Gender, sexuality and classroom materials.)

  • For: Barkman said he wanted “locally elected community representation in the hiring process” for teachers who are visible in the community through sports and concerts, because trustees answer directly to residents.
  • Against: Funk called it “an overstep of the board to meddle in what’s operational.” Superintendent Amos told the board “It is not the role of the board to do that hiring.” The Hanover Teachers’ Association later wrote to the board about the motion; the board acknowledged the letter and “No further discussion took place.”

Trustees once hired everyone: Falk recalled in 2026 that “trustees were part of the hiring process from superintendents to teachers to janitors” until the division grew too large. In March 2025 the board approved a written “P-GE-100 Approved Hiring Procedure” 6–1, to be maintained by senior administration. The procedure is not in the minutes, and the minutes record no vote reversing the 2024 motion.

A complaint to the minister

On Apr 25, 2024 the Hanover Parent Alliance for Diversity, a group whose members do not give their names, wrote to Education Minister Nello Altomare. It called for the resignation of six trustees, the appointment of a provincial official to oversee board meetings, and a review of recent motions, alleging that the hiring motion and other decisions were discriminatory. These were allegations. The minister called the trustees to a meeting, saying he wanted to know “how this improves student learning and outcomes.” He removed no one. The full story of the complaint, the counter-petition and the board’s May 3 statement is in Gender, sexuality and classroom materials.

“Real, potential or perceived”: conflict-of-interest rules

Through 2023 and 2024 the governance committee, chaired by Trustee Danielle Funk, rewrote both conflict-of-interest policies so they would match. The employee policy, GP, passed easily in March 2024, came back for changes, and on Jun 4 Funk withdrew it to take it back to the Governance Committee. The trustees’ policy, BC, passed first reading 6–3 the same night. On Jun 27 GP passed 7–1 and BC passed 5–3. According to Steinbach Online, Shayne Barkman voted against GP, and Lynn Barkman, Shayne Barkman and Jeff Friesen voted against BC.

The argument was over the words “potential or perceived”.

  • Remove them: Trustee Cheryl Froese asked “how can we discipline people for something that people perceive?” Trustee Lynn Barkman suggested the words be deleted because they were causing conflict on the board, and Trustee Jeff Friesen noted the employee policy did not use them.
  • Keep them: Funk said the policy is about awareness, not discipline: “we have to hold ourselves to extremely high standards when we’re in this position”. Superintendent Amos said the wording came from the division’s HR manager and the Manitoba School Boards Association.

Why the province appointed an adviser

On Aug 28, 2024 the division announced that Kelly Barkman, a retired superintendent of the River East Transcona and Red River divisions who had taught and been a principal in Hanover for 21 years, had been appointed by the minister “to serve in an advisory role to the Board, with funding provided by the Province.” The board’s public minutes contain no motion about the appointment.

Outside the minutes

The minister would not say specifically what prompted him to appoint a special advisor, the Carillon reported, noting the April complaint. Altomare said Barkman’s experience could “help the board understand what their main role is and that is to create safe inclusive spaces for all students”, that the final decisions would stay with the board, and that Barkman would stay “as long as he needs to until the work is done.” Chair Brad Unger said: “I hope people see this as an opportunity. A positive.” When the adviser left in 2026, Steinbach Online wrote that he had been appointed “in response to concerns that were raised in April 2024 regarding the operation of the HSD Board of Trustees.”

Not everyone welcomed it. A Carillon “View from the Legislature” column argued that each of the province’s recent interventions in school divisions “seems to be sparked, not because of a breach in legislation or policy, but because of local actions that the NDP disagree with.”

A new chair, new senior staff, and agendas adopted by vote

In September 2024 Jeff Friesen became chair, with Dallas Wiebe as vice-chair. Friesen told the Carillon the board had “gone through some turmoil in the past and have grown closer together.” Assistant Superintendent Colin Campbell left to lead Seine River after 18 years in Hanover, and Landmark Collegiate principal Marlin Adrian replaced him.

From Oct 1, 2024 every regular meeting has opened with a motion to adopt the agenda; the published minutes before then show none. The first one carried 7–1. In 2026 Friesen said he had “re-introduced to the board the approval of agendas”, which he said had not been done for years.

2025–2026: A call for review, a suspension, and a changing of the guard

Funk asks for an outside review

On Nov 5, 2024 Trustee Danielle Funk gave notice of a motion for an independent review. The Carillon reported that she said “I noticed a continuation of habits dealing with things in the committee of the whole that should be in public,” and pointed to the turnover of senior staff. She was absent in December, so the motion came to the Jan 7, 2025 meeting. Seconded by Trustee Ron Falk, it read in part:

“The HSD Board of Trustees engages an independent external reviewer to conduct a comprehensive review of our governance practices and the effectiveness of our code of conduct. This review is essential to ensure transparency, accountability, and adherence to our core values.”

Motion 2025-011, Trustee Danielle Funk · Jan 7, 2025

The reviewer would also have examined the current board’s past compliance, reported in public, and the board would have committed to acting on every finding with a timeline. A recorded vote was requested. Funk voted for it; Shayne Barkman, Ron Falk, Jeff Friesen, Cheryl Froese, Charmaine Toews and Brad Unger voted against. Lynn Barkman and Dallas Wiebe were absent. Falk seconded the motion and voted against it; the board’s by-laws allow that, so seconding a motion does not mean supporting it.

  • For: Funk said she had asked to bring items to public meetings and been refused, and that “I don’t believe we are being as transparent as we could be. I do think we have some issues with code of conduct.”
  • Against: Chair Jeff Friesen told Steinbach Online, “I think that’s very transparent, as everything we do is public record,” pointing to public minutes, access-to-information requests and published policies, and objected to the division paying for a review. Falk said the board should be looking forward and not back. Shayne Barkman called the board “consistent, transparent, accountable, and trustworthy.” Froese said the provincial adviser could already make recommendations, and that a mentor had been provided to the board for three days.

Funk did not speak to reporters afterward: under board rules, the Carillon noted, trustees are not allowed to speak to the media without chair approval.

Leave, and a six-week suspension

Funk’s last public meeting before her leave was on Feb 4, 2025. The Carillon reports that she has been on personal leave since Feb 18. The sources disagree about whether that first leave was granted. Steinbach Online reported in May 2025 that the board “unanimously voted in support of” unpaid leave until Aug 31; the Carillon later reported her first request was denied. Unlike Trustee Shannon Friesen’s leave in 2021, which was granted by public motion, the minutes contain no motion either way; from May 2025 they list her as “(Leave)”.

On Jun 3, 2025, with six trustees present, Trustee Shayne Barkman moved and Trustee Brad Unger seconded:

“Whereas Trustee Funk did not act as prescribed under Hanover School Division Policy BF - Board Member - Code of Conduct sections A.2 & A.6(a)(b), be it resolved that the Board of Trustees impose a sanction under the Public Schools Act section 35.2(1)3 and Board Member - Code of Conduct to suspend Trustee Funk from the school board for a period of six weeks; AND FURTHER that the indemnity, under section 35.2(4), not be paid for the same six-week period…”

Motion 2025-130, “Trustee Code of Conduct” · Jun 3, 2025, carried 6–0

The suspension took effect on Sept 1, 2025. Six votes is exactly two-thirds of the nine-member board, the minimum the Act requires for a suspension. Sections A.2 and A.6 are the duties to support board decisions, to treat others respectfully, and not to discredit the board in the media or on social media. The minutes do not describe what Funk did, and Steinbach Online reported no public discussion of the motion.

Outside the minutes

Chair Jeff Friesen told the Carillon: “It was something we’ve been working with for quite a period of time, with the leave and a few things, it was time to deal with this situation”, and said Funk was not being singled out. He told Steinbach Online “these types of decisions are made to bring unity to the board.” Funk wrote on Facebook: “I have, and do, take my role as a trustee seriously.” The board’s findings are its own; they were not tested publicly.

Funk appealed to a provincial adjudicator. In September 2026 the Carillon reported that, ten months after the appeal was filed, the adjudicator ruled she was “only guilty of not supporting the legitimacy of board decisions”, that is, section A.2. We could not find the ruling itself or learn whether the sanction was changed.

A second leave request, and the attendance rule

In a closed meeting on Jan 20, 2026, the board turned down Funk’s request for further unpaid medical leave by a vote of three to two, according to the Carillon; no trustee asked for a recorded vote. Without leave, she had to attend in person at least once every three months to keep her seat. Steinbach Online reported that she “must attend a public board meeting by April of this year to keep her trustee status.” The minutes show her present on Apr 7, 2026, and at later meetings in June and September 2026.

  • The board’s reasons, as Funk later released them: Wiebe’s letter said the board’s discussion included a concern that the doctor’s note was not “very informative with no return date”, and that Steinbach voters “deserve to be represented by their elected official.”
  • Funk’s view: in her request she wrote that the earlier refusal “seems punitive and retaliatory.” A law professor and former trustee quoted by the Carillon called the denial “governance bullying”; that is her opinion, and no tribunal has ruled on it. Education Minister Tracy Schmidt said “compassion and fairness must guide these decisions.”

New by-laws, a new chair, and the adviser’s exit

In June 2025 the board passed a rewritten Policy BD, its procedural by-laws, 8–0, and a revised policy on how policies are made (BG), whose first reading passed 5–1. Steinbach Online reported that the board consulted the adviser, that the new by-laws added “divisional planning” to the subjects for closed meetings, and that a section on notices of motion was removed. In September 2025 Dallas Wiebe won the chair over Brad Unger and Ron Falk; the board did not share the vote counts.

Outside the minutes

In October 2025 a Carillon access-to-information request found that “no reports or records of any kind exist” from the adviser. Minister Tracy Schmidt said her deputy minister received regular verbal updates from Kelly Barkman; the province said he had been paid $6,297 from August 2024 to Oct 23, 2025. Chair Wiebe said Barkman had made no reports to the board and that his recommendations will not be shared.

By February 2026 Barkman had ended his contract. Schmidt said his “contributions have helped move the division forward”, including advice on governance and policy; Wiebe said “he was valued by the board.” The parents’ group said he had “effected zero change” and claimed that a parent’s request to censure Unger over December 2025 remarks was turned down; that claim is unproven. (At that meeting Unger had challenged the anonymous group to meet the board in person.) The minister would not say whether she would appoint a replacement.

Another superintendent search, 2026

In June 2026 Superintendent Joe Thiessen resigned, effective Dec 31, 2026; the division gave no reason. That spring the board had also rewritten its three superintendent policies, on duties (BB), recruitment (BBA) and performance assessment (BBB), and revised the trustee code of conduct. In July it named Prairie Rose superintendent Kevin Clace to start on or before Jan 1, 2027, and Woodlawn School principal Vince Hiebert as an assistant superintendent.

In the summer of 2026 the province went further than advice. After the board voted to stop honouring the “mature minor” designation in its schools, the Carillon reported that Minister Schmidt wrote that failing to rescind it would lead her to appoint an official trustee, someone who would take over the board’s powers. On Sept 1 the board rescinded the motion 8–1. That dispute is covered in Gender, sexuality and classroom materials.

The 2026 election

Six trustees left. Ron Falk, after 34 years, and Brad Unger, a candidate for Steinbach city council, ran for council instead; Danielle Funk, Charmaine Toews and Dallas Wiebe did not seek re-election; and Lynn Barkman lost her Steinbach seat by 21 votes (1,257 to 1,278, unofficial count). Funk said she was stepping back for personal reasons and urged residents to vote: “Democracy in our division is very important and I would love to see elections in all our wards.” Toews, in her farewell, called for “wise, respectful, professional, rational, and empathetic trustees.” Every ward had a contest, advance voting was the highest in four elections, and turnout rose to 20.4%.

What the new board inherits

The board elected on Oct 28, 2026 (Jenn Elliot, Jeff Friesen, Shayne Barkman, Jacqueline Morgan, Tess Doerksen, Josh Loewen, Jeremy Neufeld, Erin Williams and Cheryl Froese) took office in November 2026. On Nov 3 it chose Jeff Friesen as chair and Shayne Barkman as vice-chair by secret ballot.

  • A new superintendent. Kevin Clace starts by Jan 1, 2027, under superintendent policies rewritten in spring 2026. Assistant Superintendent Leanne Peters retires effective Nov 30, 2026, and Vince Hiebert joined in September.
  • Rules written by the last board. The code of conduct (revised June 2026), the procedural by-laws (June 2025), the conflict-of-interest policies (2024) and the P-GE-100 hiring procedure (2025) all carry over. The 2024 music-and-gym hiring motion has not been rescinded in the public minutes.
  • No provincial adviser. The adviser’s post ended in 2025–26, and the minister has not said whether it will be filled. Her summer 2026 warning about an official trustee was resolved when the motion was rescinded.
  • Openness questions remain. About two closed meetings a month, unpublished closed-meeting minutes, no meeting video, and a rule that trustees speak to reporters only with the chair’s permission. Critics, including the parents’ group and the Carillon, also object that the text of a policy change becomes public only after final reading. The Carillon’s editorial board wrote after the election that the last board “deliberately introduced motions without explaining them” and argued that trustees are free to explain their own votes. The board’s position, in Friesen’s words, is that “everything we do is public record.” Funk’s proposed outside review was never adopted.

About this page

Built from the board’s published minutes (Jul 2013 – Nov 2026), agendas, policies and news releases on hsd.ca, Manitoba statutes and bills, and news coverage linked in the text. See How these reports are made and Data accuracy.

What we searched, and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Searched: every motion and minutes page from Jul 2013 to Sept 2026 for code of conduct, censure, sanction, suspension, conflict of interest, Policies BA–BH, BB–BBB and GP, hiring, applicants, notice of motion, governance, in-camera, recorded vote, leave, agenda adoption, election officials, adviser, K–12 Commission and Bill 64; the newspaper items listed in the minutes’ correspondence; HSD news posts; the Public Schools Act; Steinbach Online and Carillon archives.
  • What counts: the 67 decisions are votes on the board’s own rules and conduct (code of conduct, conflict of interest, by-laws, policy-making, superintendent policies, the 2020–21 policy clean-up), the censure and suspension, trustee leave, the hiring motions and procedure, the review motion, the first agenda-adoption vote, ward boundaries and election officials. Routine adoptions of closed-meeting minutes are counted in the “How often the board meets in private” table, not listed. Trustee pay is in Budget and taxes.
  • Closed-meeting count: distinct Committee of the Whole in-camera (or “closed”) meetings whose minutes were adopted at a public meeting, grouped by the date of the closed meeting. 2013 (records start in July) and 2026 (through September) are partial and left out of the table. A closed meeting whose minutes were never adopted in public would be missed.
  • The record’s limits: HSD publishes no meeting video; minutes are brief and rarely record debate; votes are counts unless a recorded vote is requested; closed-meeting minutes are not published; policy changes are posted only after final reading. Superintendent appointments, leave decisions for Trustee Funk and the code-of-conduct investigations happened in closed session.
  • Errors in the original minutes: on Apr 2, 2024, motion 2024-072 is printed under the heading “DC - Use of Divisional Credit Cards” but its text gives second and third reading to Policy BD, at the same meeting as BD’s first reading; the Oct 1, 2024 agenda motion is numbered “2023-166”. We show both as printed. A Carillon article of Jan 11, 2025 describes Funk as vice-chair; HSD’s own release names Dallas Wiebe as vice-chair for 2024–25.
  • Couldn’t confirm: what Brad Unger’s 2017 breach was; what conduct led to Funk’s 2025 sanction, the text of the adjudicator’s ruling and whether it changed the sanction; whether Funk’s first leave was granted (Steinbach Online says yes, the Carillon says no, and the minutes are silent); the Carillon’s statement that Funk attended in August 2025 (the Aug 26, 2025 minutes list her regrets); the content of P-GE-100 and whether the 2024 music-and-gym motion is still applied; who cast the minority votes on Oct 1, 2024, Mar 4, 2025 and Jun 3, 2025, and who abstained on Apr 7, 2026; exactly when the adviser’s work ended (the Carillon says December 2025) and his total pay; the parents’ group’s claim that a request to censure Unger was turned down; 2014 vote counts and turnout; and why Superintendent Thiessen resigned.
