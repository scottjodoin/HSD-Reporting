Issue history

What students learn: programs, graduation, student services and teachers

What has the school board decided about how school works for students, from where Grade 9 is taught to how graduation is run, and how have those decisions affected the teachers and educational assistants in the classroom?

Board meetings Jul 2013 – Sept 2026 · 55 decisions in the minutes · Last updated Nov 14, 2026

In short

  • Grade 9 moved to Steinbach in 2015, and is now partly moving back out. In Dec 2014 the board voted 9–0 to move Grade 9 from Bothwell, Blumenort, Kleefeld and Mitchell to Steinbach Regional Secondary School (SRSS). With SRSS near capacity, Grade 9 students from Kleefeld and Bothwell will attend Green Valley and Niverville from 2026/27.
  • The graduation policy was revised five times since 2016, and the latest version passed 4–3. The Dec 2, 2025 policy takes the division out of grad banquets: each school and its parent grad committee now decide whether to hold one and pay for it. The minutes do not say why three trustees voted no.
  • Several classroom changes started as school requests. The board let Steinbach early-years schools switch kindergarten to full days on alternate days (2017) and let Woodlawn and Southwood keep a “balanced school day” after a two-year pilot (2020).
  • Teaching jobs were cut for three straight budgets, then restored. News reports describe about four positions cut in 2021, 16 in 2022 and 10.5 in 2023, during a provincial limit on school taxes. In 2024 the board brought some back and added 16.5.
  • Educational assistants went on strike for three weeks in Nov 2023. Wages were the sticking point. A deal was reached on Nov 20, 2023. A year later, about 93 term EAs were laid off when federal Jordan’s Principle funding stopped.
  • Most of this happened without debate in the record. Of 55 decisions on these topics, 52 passed with no votes against. Collective agreements after 2015, the Grade 9 consultations and the graduation venue do not appear as decisions in the public minutes.

Background: the rules

Manitoba sets the curriculum, the credits needed to graduate and the standards for helping students with extra needs. A school board decides how those rules play out locally: which school offers which grades, how the school day and school year are arranged, how graduation is run, and how much money goes to staff and programs. The board does this mostly through policies, which need three “readings” (votes): a first reading to put a draft on the table, then second and third readings, usually at the next meeting, to adopt it.

Student supports fall under “student services”: resource teachers, educational assistants (EAs), clinicians and counsellors. HSD’s current student services policy commits the division to “providing all students with access to appropriate educational programming” and cites Manitoba’s Appropriate Educational Programming Regulation.

Teachers belong to the Hanover Teachers’ Association (HTA), a local of the Manitoba Teachers’ Society. HSD’s EAs have been represented by CLAC Local 306 since 2005. Contract talks are handled by the board’s Negotiations Committee, mostly in closed (in-camera) meetings. Only the final ratification sometimes appears in the public minutes.

2013–2015: Grade 9 goes to Steinbach; new rules for teachers

Rural Grade 9 students move to SRSS

Until 2015, students in Bothwell, Blumenort, Kleefeld and Mitchell did Grade 9 in their home community. On Dec 2, 2014, a month after a new board took office, trustees voted 9–0 “to move Grade Nine programming from Bothwell, Blumenort, Kleefeld and Mitchell schools to SRSS for the 2015/2016 school year and ongoing.” Trustee Ruby Wiens moved it and Trustee Lynn Barkman seconded. For families, it meant 14-year-olds would ride the bus into Steinbach a year earlier than before.

The public minutes don’t record why. No report or discussion is attached, and the minutes of the closed meetings before the vote (Oct 7, Oct 21 and Nov 18, 2014) were adopted the same night but are not published. In January 2015 the board’s correspondence included a letter to parents about “Grade 9 Community Consultations”; its contents are not in the minutes. One knock-on effect does appear: in Nov 2015 the board raised SRSS’s bus mileage to cover “an additional four Grade 9 teams as a result of Divisional realignment of Grade 9.”

Before the move, the board had already approved separate start-of-year orientation days for Grade 9 and 10 at SRSS and Green Valley School (May 2014) so new high-school students could settle in before older grades arrived.

French: a Blumenort exchange, and questions about immersion

In Feb 2014 the board approved an overnight trip to Winnipeg for Blumenort School’s Grade 9 French students to replace a cancelled exchange with a school in Coaticook, Quebec.

Immersion came up in 2015 and 2016, mostly from Niverville. A resident’s letter about French immersion (J.S.) was set aside “to be discussed at the Board Planning Session”. The board’s news file that month included a Carillon story, “Niverville council calls for French immersion feedback”. In June 2016 the Education Committee reviewed a French immersion report by J.S., and that fall it looked at “French Immersion Transportation in Lieu”; the minutes give no details. No motion on French immersion appears anywhere in the minutes.

A teacher transfer policy, passed 6–2

In Feb 2015 the board gave first reading to Policy GH, Teacher Transfer, by a 6–2 vote, one of the few split votes on staff matters. A month later it passed final reading 8–0. The policy was rewritten as “Principal and Teacher Transfers” in 2018 and rescinded in Mar 2020. Its text is no longer posted, and the minutes do not say what it required or why two trustees opposed it at first.

Student services and the 2015 contracts

The board added an HR Coordinator for Student Services to the 2015/16 budget, and in Dec 2015 adopted a new Policy EA, Student Services, and a policy on dogs in schools (later service animals).

2015 was a bargaining year for both big employee groups. In June the board ratified an “Agreement in Committee” with the Hanover Teachers’ Association (the deal reached by the two bargaining teams), and in November one with CLAC Local 306 for educational assistants, both 9–0. The division announced that EAs had ratified a five-year deal, retroactive to Sept 1, 2014, with annual raises and more paid sick days. These are the only collective agreements whose ratification appears in the public minutes; later ones were settled out of public view.

2016–2019: A health clinic, longer kindergarten days and graduation rules

A health clinic inside SRSS

In Oct 2016 HSD, Southern Health-Santé Sud and Steinbach Family Medical announced a clinic inside SRSS, staffed with a full-time nurse practitioner and paid for by Southern Health and Manitoba Health. Then-superintendent Randy Dueck said students would lose less class time:

“This partnership will provide SRSS students with convenience of access to comprehensive health care, and with these services being made available on-site, the time away from classes can be greatly reduced.”

Randy Dueck, Superintendent-CEO · HSD news release, Oct 26, 2016

No board vote on the clinic appears in the public minutes. By mid-2018 Dueck reported over 200 student appointments a month. In 2019 HSD also joined a provincial pilot pairing a school social worker with health-system mental health and addictions staff, later made permanent, according to Steinbach Online.

Kindergarten: full days, every other day

In early 2017 the division surveyed parents on “all day / every other day vs. half day / every day” kindergarten. In March the board voted 7–0 to switch Steinbach’s early-years schools from half-day every day to all-day alternate days starting in 2017/18. Survey results were not published with the minutes.

School nutrition

The board adopted Policy CE, School Nutrition, in Feb 2017. The current version says healthy food choices should “predominate” in cafeterias, breakfast and lunch programs, canteens, vending machines, snack programs and fundraisers. Each school writes its own procedures.

Graduation: Saturday ceremonies and a 2019 rulebook

SRSS holds its graduation at Winnipeg’s convention centre, and the venue’s calendar has often set the date. The board moved SRSS graduation to a Saturday three years running: Jun 21, 2014, Jun 27, 2015 and Jun 25, 2016, “due to facility availability.” Teachers who worked at least three hours that Saturday got a day off in lieu the next week. In 2019 it raised the SRSS banquet allowance by $5 a plate “due to the cost of the venue”.

The graduation policy, Policy JE, was revised in June 2016 and again in Aug 2019, both unanimously. The 2019 version (an archived copy is here) said graduates were entitled to “the convocation and banquet supported by the Hanover School Division”. Both events had to be on the same day. SRSS would graduate on the second-last day of classes, and the three rural high schools the day after. The division helped pay for the banquet, ceremony, diplomas and gowns, and the guest list included trustees, principals, teachers and senior staff “and their spouses.”

The provincial education review

In 2019 Manitoba’s Commission on K–12 Education held public hearings; Education Minister Kelvin Goertzen said it received between 15,000 and 20,000 submissions. The superintendent reported to the board in April on a “Student Invitation” to the Commission, and the board later referred to its own recommendations submitted to the Commission in 2019. The fight over the resulting Bill 64, which would have abolished elected school boards, is covered in Board governance.

2020–2022: Balanced days, pandemic graduations and the first teaching cuts

The balanced school day

In late 2017 two Steinbach elementary schools asked to change their timetable (“Woodlawn/Southwood Timetable Request”). Instead of two short recesses and a lunch hour, Woodlawn and Southwood ran two 25-minute nutrition and activity breaks as a two-year pilot. Assistant Superintendent Shelley Amos said “the overwhelming response has been positive.” On Mar 3, 2020 the board voted 7–0 to let both schools continue indefinitely with the Balanced School Day. Trustee Danielle Funk, who chaired the Education Committee and had children at the schools, told Steinbach Online:

“In my kids, I’ve noticed a change in behavior after school… and they are less tired and less hungry.”

Danielle Funk, trustee · Steinbach Online, Mar 11, 2020

Student services and graduation policy changes

The same spring the board rescinded the teacher transfer policy and adopted an amended Policy EA, Student Services Management. In May 2021 it rescinded Policy JEA, Graduation Credit Requirement, 8–0. The minutes do not say what that policy required or why it was dropped.

Graduation in a pandemic

COVID-19 rules ended traditional ceremonies in 2020 and 2021. SRSS held its first-ever drive-in convocation in a church parking lot in June 2020, and the rural high schools did the same. The 2021 ceremonies used drive-in grads and a big screen again. In 2022 SRSS returned to the RBC Convention Centre. Superintendent Shelley Amos said that because SRSS is so large, “there’s limited options for us.” Three years running, the board approved graduation dates that “fall outside standard HSD policy” on an exception basis: for June 2022, June 2023 and June 2024. The pandemic years are covered in more detail on the COVID-19 page.

Treaty land acknowledgement and Indigenous education

In Nov 2021 the board unanimously adopted Policy AI, Treaty Land Acknowledgement. It says division staff “may read” the acknowledgement at board meetings, graduations and other events, and that a plaque will be placed in every building. The statement reads in part:

“We acknowledge the traditional territory upon which the schools of Hanover School Division reside as the lands of Treaty 1 territory and the homeland of the Metis people.”

Policy AI, Treaty Land Acknowledgement · adopted Nov 2, 2021

Indigenous education otherwise shows up in the minutes as Education Committee reports (“Indigenous Education Initiatives”, 2017; “Indigenous Education”, 2023), not motions. School-level efforts have made the news. Elmdale School was one of only 50 Manitoba schools to receive a Métis education kit in 2020. Niverville High School gave Indigenous graduates ribbon stoles in 2024, when eight students signed up. In 2024 the division was hiring a K-12 Indigenous education learning coach.

Nutrition policy updated

The board revised Policy CE, School Nutrition in Dec 2021. In 2022 the province added $1.3 million for school meal programs run through the Child Nutrition Council of Manitoba.

The first teaching cuts

From 2021 to 2023 the province froze, then limited, the school taxes boards could raise. In 2023 the division wrote that provincial guidelines no longer allowed divisions to raise additional funds through taxation (see Budget and taxes). In 2021 the board cut two learning coach positions and about two full-time teaching positions. Finance Chair Rick Peters said, “Anytime you touch the classroom it is a difficult decision to make.” In 2022, with fewer students, 16 term-teacher positions were not renewed. Peters said the reduction “will not impact classroom sizes”.

2023–2024: Graduation rewritten, an attendance policy and an EA strike

The graduation policy, twice in one year

The board amended Policy JE twice in 2023. The first change passed first reading on Mar 7 by 8–1, with all nine trustees present, and final reading on Apr 4 by 8–0 with one abstention. Between those votes the Carillon ran a story the board listed as “Board chair calls out ‘cheap’ grad policy”; we could not find the article itself. The second change passed in October and November, unanimously.

Comparing the 2019 policy with the November 2023 version shows two changes:

  • Dates. SRSS would now graduate “within seven calendar days of the last day of school division classes,” with the other three high schools after SRSS and before the last day. This replaced the fixed “second last day” rule the board had been waiving each year.
  • Spouses pay their own way. Trustees, principals, teachers and senior staff were still invited to the banquet, but “Spouses may be invited to the banquet, however they will bear the cost of their ticket.”

The minutes do not say which change came in April and which in November. The April 2023 version of the file was not archived.

An attendance policy

In Nov 2023 the board unanimously adopted Policy JI, Student Presence and Engagement, its first attendance policy in the record. Attendance had been a provincial priority for some time; in 2020 Manitoba set up an attendance task force. The policy defines “chronic absenteeism” (for example, unexcused absences for 10% or more of school days in K–8). It calls for a student-specific plan, says the division will respond in a “non-punitive, fair and predictable manner, eliminating suspensions, expulsions and withdrawals” for absences, and says severe cases are reported to the province.

Smaller supports

In Feb 2023 the board waived rental fees so Manitoba Possible could offer sign language courses to parents of hearing impaired children in a division classroom in the evenings. In Jan 2024 Student Services presented its F.I.R.E. (Fun, Inclusive, Recreational Events) program. As enrolment grew, partly through immigration, a provincial newcomer grant paid for another full-time EAL teacher (English as an Additional Language) in 2023.

The 2023 budget: fewer teachers, more students

For 2023/24 the board faced a $2.7 million gap. At the public budget meeting, Finance Chair Danielle Funk warned that cutting teachers “will result in larger class sizes, less help for students with additional needs, and fewer course options.” The final budget cut 10.5 teaching positions. It also did not add the 15 positions normally budgeted for about 279 more students, and it ended the summer reading program and Grade 2 swimming lessons, according to Steinbach Online. The budget itself is covered in Budget and taxes.

The educational assistants’ strike

EAs support students with extra needs and help supervise recess. Their last contract expired in the fall of 2022, and talks began in March 2023. In October, 96% voted in favour of strike action. On Nov 1, 2023 nearly 300 EAs walked off the job.

The two sides disagreed about money. CLAC regional director Geoff Dueck Thiessen said the division’s low overhead had come at EAs’ expense:

“For a long time, Hanover School Division has had the lowest administrative costs per student in the province… This has translated into precarious wages for their EAs, who are paid several dollars per hour less than those in directly neighbouring school divisions.”

Geoff Dueck Thiessen, CLAC · Steinbach Online, Oct 31, 2023

The division said its offer, 13% over four years with bigger raises at the bottom of the scale, was the largest in recent history. It said the union’s proposal was more than 20%, and that labour costs already took 83% of its budget. Trustee Danielle Funk, chair of the Negotiations Committee, said:

“We are not able to pay everybody because we get less funding for our students.”

Danielle Funk, trustee · Steinbach Online, Nov 2, 2023

During the strike, schools asked parents to volunteer for recess supervision, and the division hired temporary support aides. A parent, V.C., whose child relies on an EA, objected that “There should be no job postings for this whatsoever.” Superintendent Shelley Amos said the temporary hires included “all required self-declaration and safety checks.” Board Chair Brad Unger announced that a tentative settlement had been reached on Nov 20, with a ratification vote on Nov 22. The terms were not published in the minutes. The board’s news file lists stories headlined “EAs celebrate a return to school” and “EAs agree to new four-year contract”.

2024: universal school meals and teachers restored

In early 2024 the province told divisions about its “Universally Accessible School Nutrition Programming”. HSD built a two-tier lunch program with its program sponsor, Soup’s On: grab-and-go fruit and vegetables available to all students, plus full breakfasts and lunches for students with higher needs. Assistant Superintendent Marlin Adrian said the aim was to feed students “in a way that’s stigma-free.”

After the province restored school boards’ authority to raise local taxes, the 2024/25 budget brought back “at least eight or eight and a half” of the cut teaching positions and added 16.5 more for growth. Finding substitutes remained hard. In Feb 2024 HSD had 558 teachers and a list of 167 substitutes, and by 2025 it accepted substitutes with any recognized degree or an Early Childhood Educator certificate, “no education degree required.” The board’s 2024 change to how teachers are hired is covered in Board governance.

2025–2026: EA layoffs, a new graduation policy and Grade 9 moves again

EA layoffs after Jordan’s Principle funding stopped

Jordan’s Principle is a federal program that pays for supports for First Nations children. HSD had used it for three years, and the EAs it paid for were term staff. In Nov 2024 the division laid off approximately 93 educational assistants. The program had moved from provincial to federal administration in October, and HSD was told its September invoice would not be paid. Superintendent Joe Thiessen said HSD was seeking to recoup about $1 million in wages already paid, and stressed:

“This layoff notice has nothing to do with job performance.”

Joe Thiessen, Superintendent · Steinbach Online, Nov 28, 2024

In January the board received a letter from the federal government denying funding.

Student services, meals and service animals

The nutrition policy was revised again in April 2025 to say schools can draw on the Universal Accessible School Nutrition Program through the division. The student services policy got a first reading in June 2025 but no final vote. It was restarted and adopted in March 2026 under the new title “Appropriate Education”. The service animal policy was revised in 2025 and again in June 2026. All passed unanimously.

The Dec 2025 graduation policy (4–3)

On Dec 2, 2025, with seven trustees present, the board passed a new graduation policy 4–3. Trustee Jeff Friesen moved it and Trustee Charmaine Toews seconded. First reading a month earlier had been 7–0, also with seven present. The two meetings had different absences, so the counts don’t show who changed position. The vote was not recorded by name, and no news report explains the three votes against.

The new policy takes the banquet out of the division’s role. Graduates are now entitled to the convocation only, and the division’s financial help covers “ceremony, diplomas, and gowns”; the banquet is gone from that list. A new clause says:

“The decision to hold a graduation banquet rests solely with the school and their Parent Grad Committee. The financial obligations related to the banquet are the sole responsibility of these parties.”

Policy JE, Graduation · last reviewed Dec 2, 2025

The rule that the ceremony and banquet be held on one day was also dropped. Board Chair Dallas Wiebe described the goal as local choice:

“We want to give flexibility to principals and parent councils (and have them) make their own decisions for their own community and graduation.”

Dallas Wiebe, board chair · Steinbach Online, Dec 11, 2025

The news report and the posted policy don’t fully match. Steinbach Online wrote that schools now have “the freedom to choose dates and times”. It also quoted Wiebe saying the banquet subsidy “is still there for them” if a school holds one. The policy as posted still has the SRSS seven-day rule and the order of the other schools’ ceremonies, and it lists banquet costs as the school’s and grad committee’s sole responsibility. We could not confirm how any banquet subsidy is now set.

SRSS graduation stays in Winnipeg

Some parents hoped SRSS graduation would move to Steinbach’s new Southeast Event Centre. In 2026 the division kept it at the RBC Convention Centre. Superintendent Joe Thiessen said that year’s ceremony drew more than 3,600 people for 515 graduates, and that the Steinbach proposal would not be able to accommodate all components of graduation (parking, ceremony, banquet and Safe Grad) in one place on one day. He also said it would cost families more. “I presented all the facts to the board,” Thiessen said; the board agreed. No public motion on the venue appears in the minutes. Across the division, 713 students graduated in 2026.

Grade 9 moves again, and new programs

Eleven years after Grade 9 was centralized at SRSS, the division said the school was at or near capacity. On Dec 2, 2025, the day of the meeting that passed the graduation policy, the division announced that the board had approved changes: from 2026/27, Grade 9 students from Kleefeld will go to Green Valley School (which becomes Grades 7–12) and those from Bothwell to Niverville High School. The published minutes of that meeting record no vote on the changes. Blumenort students may request a change to Landmark Collegiate. Students from any catchment may still apply to SRSS vocational programs from Grade 10, space permitting. The growth behind this is covered in Schools and growth.

SRSS also launched a dual-credit health care aide program with RRC Polytech in 2025/26, in which students earn high school and college credits at the same time. More than 40 students were interested; 17 enrolled.

Other 2026 items

The Treaty Land Acknowledgement policy was on the Governance Committee’s agenda in May, early June and late June 2026, with no motion yet. In June 2026 the board voted 6–0 that the “mature minor” category “is not to be honored in any HSD building”, which affects whether older students can consent to their own health care. After the Minister of Education issued a directive requiring it to do so, the board rescinded that motion 8–1 on Sept 1, 2026. The minutes do not say whether the motion was aimed at the SRSS health clinic. That dispute is outside this page’s scope.

What the new board inherits

The board elected on Oct 28, 2026 (Jenn Elliot, Jeff Friesen, Shayne Barkman, Jacqueline Morgan, Tess Doerksen, Josh Loewen, Jeremy Neufeld, Erin Williams and Cheryl Froese) took office in November 2026. From the record:

  • Graduation: the Dec 2025 policy leaves banquets to each school and its parent grad committee. The gap between the posted policy and the board chair’s description of dates and subsidies is not resolved in the record.
  • Grade 9 catchments change this school year (2026/27) for Kleefeld and Bothwell, with Green Valley becoming a Grades 7–12 school and an optional move for Blumenort students to Landmark.
  • EA bargaining: CLAC sent a notice of intent to bargain for EAs in spring 2026, so a new EA contract is due during this board’s term.
  • Treaty Land Acknowledgement policy: under committee review since May 2026, with no motion yet.
  • Funding-dependent programs: the universal lunch program and the student mental health team depend on provincial funding, and the Jordan’s Principle EA positions on federal funding. The record does not show whether the roughly $1 million in Jordan’s Principle wages was recovered.

About this page

Built from the board’s published minutes (Jul 2013 – Sept 2026), agendas, policies and news releases on hsd.ca, archived copies of earlier versions of the graduation policy, and news coverage linked in the text. See How these reports are made and Data accuracy.

What we searched, and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Searched: all 1,660 motions and the full text of every set of minutes and agendas for: French, immersion, Grade 9 / Grade Nine, graduation, grad, JE, JEA, JI, attendance, kindergarten, balanced school day, student services, Policy EA, EAA, inclusion, clinic, nutrition, Treaty, Indigenous, Jordan’s Principle, teacher transfer, GH, HTA, CLAC, collective agreement, negotiations, Manitoba Possible and K–12 Commission. We also read the news-clipping lists in the minutes, the current policies JE, JI, CE, AI and EA, and the archived 2019 and November 2023 versions of Policy JE. Outside news was read on Steinbach Online and hsd.ca.
