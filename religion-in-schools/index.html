Issue history

Religion in Hanover schools: prayer, religious instruction and parent petitions

Manitoba public schools must be non-sectarian, yet many Hanover schools hold morning prayers and weekly religion classes. How does that work, who decides, which schools have them, and how has the board handled the yearly petitions since 2013?

Board meetings Jul 2013 – Sept 2026 · 75 decisions in the minutes · Last updated Nov 14, 2026

In short

  • Parents decide, the board must follow. Under the Public Schools Act, a petition from the parents of 60 students brings optional morning religious exercises to a school, and a petition for 25 students obliges the board to pass a by-law allowing religious instruction. Only children whose parents sign up take part. Every May or June the board passes both; the minutes show no qualifying petition turned down.
  • Instruction is steady; morning exercises have shrunk. Religious instruction was authorized in 15 to 17 schools every year in the record (16 schools for 2026/27). Religious exercises went from 17 or 18 schools a year between 2015 and 2020 to 9 schools for 2026/27. The minutes don’t say why.
  • Late petitions were usually accepted. The board’s April 30 deadline is its own, not the province’s. It added schools after its main spring vote in seven years (2015, 2016, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2024). The one split vote on this topic was in 2015: Mitchell Middle School’s late petition carried 8–1, with Ruby Wiens against.
  • 2019 and 2020 tested the system. Four schools missed the 2019 deadline and briefly lost their exercises before the board reinstated them. In 2020 the pandemic pushed the deadline to July 31, and by-laws said programs would start when pandemic rules allowed.
  • 2026 brought a policy review and a neutrality debate. The board kept its 1984 Origin of Life policy, which tells teachers to present “different concepts” on the origin of life, and re-adopted its religious exercises policy. A parents’ group told the board it opposed the province’s anti-Islamophobia action plan, saying it wanted religious neutrality in schools.

Background: the rules

Manitoba’s Public Schools Act says “Public schools shall be non-sectarian,” meaning they are not run by or for any one church. The same Act allows two kinds of religious activity, but only when parents ask for it in writing, school by school, every year.

Religious exercises: a short daily devotion

If the parents or guardians of at least 60 students (or of 75% of students in a school with fewer than 80) sign a petition, religious exercises “shall be conducted” for those parents’ children that school year. In Hanover this has meant a few minutes before classes start. Citing Superintendent Shelley Amos, the Carillon reported in 2020: “Usually a verse is read, a hymn sang and the Lord’s Prayer recited.”

The province’s guide for educators explains that in 1992 the Court of Queen’s Bench ruled mandatory religious exercises violated the Charter of Rights and Freedoms and struck down most of that part of the Act, leaving only the non-sectarian rule and the petition. The older subsections on how exercises were run are now gone from the Act: the current consolidation shows subsections 84(2) to (7) as repealed by S.M. 2025, c. 47.

Provincial guidance, which HSD repeats on its religious exercises and instruction page, says participation “must be an opt-in process”, schools and boards must not start or promote petitions, staff cannot be required to lead exercises, the petitioners supply any materials, and exercises are held each teaching day for no longer than ten minutes, before classes or at noon. Any faith group can petition.

Religious instruction: a weekly class

If the parents of at least 25 children in a school with three or more classrooms (10 in a one- or two-room school) petition, the board “shall pass a by-law” allowing religious instruction there. The Act allows it during school hours, up to 2½ hours a week, taught by clergy or people they authorize, and any student whose parents object must be excused. In Hanover the classes are run by an outside group: citing Amos, the Carillon reported that religious instruction “is conducted by Manitoba-based organization Discovery Time.”

What a by-law reading is. A by-law is a formal local law. The board must give it three readings (votes), normally at separate meetings. Each year the board gives the religious instruction by-law first reading, votes to “waive the standard one meeting delay”, and then gives second and third readings the same night, so the by-law is in place before the school year. That is why each year shows three instruction motions plus one exercises motion.

The division’s own policies

Hanover’s Policy JC, Religious Exercises & Instruction, first written in 1995, adds local rules. Petitions are due at the division office by April 30; for exercises, “Late submissions will be reviewed at the discretion of the Board.” No staff or trustee may help with petitions. Exercises are held before the school day, led by volunteers, and never broadcast on the PA system or held in an assembly; students who don’t take part but arrive early on the bus wait in a supervised classroom. Instruction is limited to “a single half-hour class per week”, well under the Act’s limit. Volunteer instructors need a criminal record check and a Child Abuse Registry check, and students who opt out work on the regular curriculum without being given extra homework or busy work.

A second policy, Policy JB, Origin of Life Instruction (1984), says the origin of life “should be treated in an objective, balanced manner”, directs teachers “to present an overview of the different concepts relating to the origin of life”, and asks principals to obtain “up-to-date materials that present evidence in support of alternative views concerning the origin of life.”

2013–2018: A yearly routine, and the first arguments over deadlines

The board’s own meetings opened with prayer until 2016

When the published record begins, each board meeting opened with “Scripture & Prayer” led by a trustee or senior staff member, later listed as “Opening & Prayer”. The last meeting with a prayer item was Dec 13, 2016; from Jan 10, 2017 the item is simply “Opening”. The minutes record no motion or reason for the change.

A predictable spring vote

On May 6, 2014 the board passed By-Law No. 4/2014, allowing religious instruction in 15 schools for 2014/15, and authorized religious exercises in 15 schools, all 9–0. The same pattern repeated each spring. Instruction was requested mainly for elementary and middle schools; exercises also ran at Landmark Collegiate and Steinbach Regional Secondary School. Local churches have helped parents organize the petitions: in 2017 Pastor Dave Dickey, co-chair of the Steinbach and Area Ministerial, told Steinbach Online that only one parent or guardian per family may sign, and that the work “does take some parents that really believe in this to speak up and to work along with their local church.” In 2018 Superintendent Randy Dueck stressed the division’s hands-off role:

“This is never driven by the schools, it can’t be driven by the schools. It’s driven by the communities…”

Randy Dueck, superintendent, to Steinbach Online · May 6, 2018
Outside the minutes

In 2015 the question was live elsewhere in Manitoba. The Winnipeg School Division board split over “lunchtime Bible studies and preschool prayer” run by Child Evangelism Fellowship, and its trustees suggested school boards “should have a say” in which organizations are approved. In neighbouring Seine River School Division, trustees approved a request involving the same group; the Carillon’s story began, “Their hands may be tied by the Public Schools Act…” Hanover’s board received a Free Press article, “Gospel lessons in secular schools fought”, and a Manitoba School Boards Association email on religious exercises and instruction in April 2015, then an MSBA email listed as “Minister Bjornson PKG re Religious Instruction and Religious Exercises” in May 2015. Their contents are not in the minutes.

May 2015: the policy is updated

On May 5, 2015 the board gave final reading to an updated Policy JC, 8–0, and passed that year’s by-law and exercises motion for 16 schools. The minutes do not describe what changed in the policy.

June 2015: a late petition from Mitchell Middle School

Mitchell Middle School was missing from the May list. On Jun 2, 2015 the board discussed “the MMS PAC request to extend the deadline” from the school’s parent advisory council and authorized exercises there 8–1. Trustee Ruby Wiens asked for a recorded vote: everyone else voted for it, and she voted against. According to the Carillon, the school’s petition had 60 signatures “but not enough were valid”, and parents sent more after noticing the error. The Carillon reported both sides:

“It’s understandable that sometimes things are missed but I’m very uncomfortable with waiving one of our six guidelines.”

Trustee Ruby Wiens, who voted against, to the Carillon · Jun 3, 2015

“I think the more we play around with our deadlines and what we have set for policies, the more we may be targeted.”

Trustee Cyndy Friesen, who voted in favour but questioned the deadline, as quoted by the Carillon · Jun 3, 2015

Friesen also told the board “We know people are watching,” referring to people who want religion out of schools. The Carillon noted that trustees “legally could not have rejected the petition” if it met the Act’s requirements, and that board chair Ron Falk suggested the deadline “may be reconsidered at the committee level.” The April 30 deadline is still in the policy. A year later Mitchell Middle School was again approved separately, on Jun 7, 2016, this time 9–0.

2016: the origin-of-life policy

In February 2016 Policy JB, Origins of Life, came to the board and “will be brought forward to a future meeting for further discussion.” On Mar 1, 2016 the board gave final reading to both Policy JB and Policy JC, 9–0. The minutes do not record the discussion.

Outside the minutes

Teaching about religions is different from religious instruction. In 2017 the Carillon described a Grade 12 world religions course at Green Valley School in Grunthal, a school-initiated course approved by the province, where students must “attend at least one service at a faith group different than their own.” For the division’s regular courses, see Learning programs.

2019–2020: Missed signatures, then a pandemic

May 2019: four schools lose their morning exercises

On May 7, 2019 the board authorized exercises in 14 schools. Green Valley, Niverville High, Landmark Collegiate and Landmark Elementary were missing, and Landmark Collegiate was also missing from the instruction by-law. Superintendent Dueck told Steinbach Online it was “a first for us”:

“…we cannot go out and canvas for these kinds of things, it has to come from the community. This year, four schools have not received that permission.”

Randy Dueck, superintendent, to Steinbach Online · May 9, 2019

Church leaders were surprised. Pastor John Neufeld of Bergthaler Church in Grunthal said “we were very confident we had a surplus of signatures”, and Pastor Paul Martens of Landmark Christian Fellowship asked the board to recount, saying “I’m hoping there can be a reversal.” CTV News, the Canadian Press and the Carillon also covered the story, along with a Carillon editorial titled “Petition requirements should be respected” (listed in the board’s June 2019 correspondence).

June 2019: the board waives its deadline

On Jun 4, 2019 the board voted 9–0 to authorize exercises at the four schools and passed a second instruction by-law for Landmark Collegiate. That brought 2019/20 to 18 schools with exercises, the most in the record. According to Steinbach Online, Superintendent Dueck said each community “did have the required 60 parental signatures” and had only been late. Board chair Ron Falk explained:

“In some of the communities, there was a turnover of people involved with leading the petitions and some information didn’t get passed on soon enough, in other communities the effort was too last minute.”

Ron Falk, board chair, to Steinbach Online · Jun 5, 2019

Falk called it “a bit of an anomaly”, and Dueck said it could “serve as a reminder to the communities that it is their responsibility to make sure these petitions are in.” The Carillon reported that Falk had pointed out the April 30 deadline is set by the board “and not something mandated by the province.”

2020: two deadline extensions

With physical-distancing rules in place because of COVID-19, Steinbach Online reported in May 2020 that no community had enough signatures by April 30 and that the board had “pushed back the April 30th deadline to June 19th,” with digital petitions to be accepted. Chair Rick Peters said “we knew the gathering of signatures was going to be difficult.” That first extension does not appear as a motion in the published minutes. On Jun 2, 2020 the board voted 9–0 to extend the deadline to July 31, 2020 “due to the COVID-19 pandemic.” Peters told Steinbach Online, “We had a letter from the Minister of Education suggesting that, given the circumstances, we move our deadline to July 31st.”

Fall 2020: approved, but on hold

On Aug 25, 2020 the board passed the by-law for 15 schools and authorized exercises in 16 schools, adding a new line: programs “will commence at such a time and in such a manner that logistical requirements related to the pandemic can be addressed.” On Sept 1, 2020 it added Landmark Collegiate for both. The minutes record 9–0 votes on the Landmark Collegiate by-law, but no vote on its exercises motion: the page goes straight to adjournment. Also, the minutes’ wording of that night’s motion to waive the one-meeting delay names By-Law No. 5/2020, though the item title is By-Law No. 6/2020.

“Obviously we are not going to have religious instructions or exercises if we cannot get together in groups and we have to keep the cohorts together.”

Rick Peters, board chair, to CHVN Radio · Sept 1, 2020

The province then gave divisions more flexibility. Interim Superintendent Shelley Amos told Steinbach Online, “We can now hold it in a different space than it normally is held in and we can hold it at different times throughout the day,” and that larger schools could hold exercises fewer times a week. For COVID-19 rules in general, see COVID-19.

2021–2025: Fewer schools ask for morning exercises

2021 and 2022: both policies amended

In March and April 2021 the board gave first and final readings to an “amended Policy JB” and an amended Policy JC (final readings Apr 6, 2021, 7–0). It amended Policy JC again in 2022 (first reading Mar 1, final reading Apr 5). Only the current versions are public, so this page can’t say what changed.

2021: a gap in the record

The agenda for the Jun 29, 2021 meeting lists By-Law No. 4/2021 on religious instruction and that year’s exercises motion. The board adopted the minutes of that meeting on Aug 31, 2021, but those minutes are not among the minutes HSD publishes, so which schools were approved for 2021/22 is unknown. Two more schools were added that fall, each with the pandemic clause: Kleefeld School on Oct 5 and Mitchell Middle School on Nov 2, both 9–0.

2022 to 2025: instruction steady, exercises fewer

In May 2022 the instruction by-law covered 16 schools, including Steinbach Regional Secondary School for the first time in the record, but exercises were authorized at only 6 schools: Blumenort, Bothwell, Kleefeld, Mitchell Elementary, Steinbach Regional Secondary and Woodlawn. The number with exercises rose to 12 for 2023/24, then fell to 10 and then 9, where it stayed for 2026/27. Clearspring Middle, Elmdale, Southwood and Niverville Elementary, which had exercises every year from 2014/15 to 2020/21, are not on any exercises list from 2022/23 on. Niverville Elementary and Niverville Middle also drop off the instruction by-law from 2025/26, while the new Parkhill School joins it.

Schools kept being added after the main vote, all unanimously: Mitchell Middle School on Aug 29, 2023, and Mitchell Elementary and Mitchell Middle on Jun 27, 2024. In between, a parent started an online petition on Jun 11, 2024 urging HSD to “reinstate religious opening exercises for the upcoming school year” at the two Mitchell schools.

2026: Policy reviews and a debate about neutrality

April 2026: Policy JC reopened, Policy JB held back

On Apr 7, 2026 the board gave first reading to Policy JC by 6–0 with one trustee abstaining (the minutes don’t say who). Policy JB, Origin of Life Instruction, was listed just above it with no motion. According to the Carillon, JB “was tabled to be discussed at a later date.”

Outside the minutes

The Carillon found that four neighbouring divisions (Seine River, Border Land, Red River Valley and Sunrise) have no similar policy, according to its own analysis. Asked how the policy affects classrooms, board chair Dallas Wiebe read the policy aloud and said “Specific resources would be up-to-date materials from the principal.” Laura Reimer, a school board governance consultant, said the policy is likely “not anchored in the Manitoba curriculum”, called it neither harmful nor relevant, and said that “the board is doing its due diligence” by reviewing it.

May 5, 2026: a delegation on the anti-Islamophobia plan

At the May 5, 2026 meeting a delegation presented “opposition to the provincially provided anti-islamophobia action plan”, which the minutes summarize as “Seeking neutrality in schools accepting all religions but promoting no one religion over another on a non-sectarian basis.” The province released its kindergarten-to-Grade-12 plan in December 2025, according to the Carillon; a Steinbach Online story on HSD putting it in place, with a new Holocaust curriculum, had been listed in the board’s February correspondence.

“Our concern is focused, basically, on one principle, that is maintaining religious neutrality, as well as (a) non-sectarian education system in public schools.”

J.B., spokesperson for a group of Hanover parents, to the Carillon · May 19, 2026
Outside the minutes

The spokesperson told the Carillon “we are not here to criticize any religion” and that parents mainly wanted to know when and how the plan would be used. A provincial spokesperson said the plan is “meant to be used as an anti-hate teaching tool or resource for instances of Islamophobia in schools” rather than part of the regular curriculum. The Carillon reported that no trustee answered the implementation question that night and that the board said it would have an answer “at the next meeting.”

Later that evening the board voted 8–0 “To affirm existing Policy JB - Origin of Life Instruction” and 8–0 to give final reading to Policy JC. Both policies now show May 5, 2026 as their last review. The current Policy JC is the version described in the background above.

June 2026: the yearly vote

On Jun 2, 2026 the board passed By-Law No. 2/2026, allowing religious instruction in 16 schools for 2026/27, and authorized exercises in 9: Blumenort, Green Valley, Kleefeld, Landmark Collegiate, Landmark Elementary, Mitchell Elementary, Mitchell Middle, South Oaks and Steinbach Regional Secondary. All four motions passed 6–0. The same meeting’s correspondence lists a Steinbach Online story on parents seeking clarity about the anti-Islamophobia plan, but the published minutes through September 2026 record no board answer to the delegation.

What the new board inherits

The board elected on Oct 28, 2026 (Jenn Elliot, Jeff Friesen, Shayne Barkman, Jacqueline Morgan, Tess Doerksen, Josh Loewen, Jeremy Neufeld, Erin Williams and Cheryl Froese) took office in November 2026. On this topic it inherits:

  • This school year’s authorizations. Religious instruction is authorized in 16 schools and religious exercises in 9 for 2026/27, under By-Law No. 2/2026 and the June 2026 exercises motion.
  • The next petition round. Under Policy JC, petitions for 2027/28 are due at the division office by April 30, 2027, and the board has passed the by-law and exercises motion in May or June every year. If a petition meets the Act’s numbers, the board has no choice but to approve it; its discretion is over late petitions.
  • Two policies just reviewed. Policy JC and Policy JB were both confirmed on May 5, 2026. Three members of the new board, Jeff Friesen, Shayne Barkman and Cheryl Froese, also served on the board that made those decisions.
  • An open question from parents. The May 2026 delegation asked how and when the province’s anti-Islamophobia action plan will be used in Hanover schools; no answer appears in the published minutes through September 2026.

  • Searched: every motion, minutes page and agenda from Jul 2013 to Sept 2026 for “religio”, “petition”, “prayer”, “Bible”, “Gideon”, “chaplain”, “origin(s) of life”, “creation”, “faith”, “church”, “sectarian”, “neutrality” and “Islamophobia”; the current Policies JC and JB; HSD’s religious exercises page; the Public Schools Act; the province’s guide Responding to Religious Diversity in Manitoba’s Schools; and Steinbach Online, CHVN Radio and Carillon coverage. No mention of Gideon Bibles or school chaplains was found in the HSD record.
  • What counts: the 75 decisions are the yearly religious instruction by-law readings, religious exercises authorizations, the 2020 deadline extension, and the votes on Policies JC and JB. Field trips to Bible camps, church building rentals and leases, and the 2016 staff “Alternate Religious Holidays” agreement (handled in a closed meeting) are left out.
  • The record’s limits: HSD publishes no meeting video; minutes are brief and record vote counts, not who voted which way, except for recorded votes; closed (in-camera) meeting minutes are not published. School counts come from the school names listed in each motion.
  • Couldn’t confirm: which schools were approved on Jun 29, 2021 (those minutes are not published); what changed in Policies JC and JB in 2015, 2016, 2021 and 2022 (only current versions are public); why the board stopped opening its meetings with prayer in 2017; the vote on the Sept 1, 2020 Landmark Collegiate exercises motion; the board’s first 2020 extension to June 19, reported by Steinbach Online but not in the minutes; the Carillon’s 2015 report that two schools’ late petitions were excused in 2014 (no 2014 motion shows this); whether exercises and instruction actually resumed in 2020/21; how many students take part; and why fewer schools have petitioned for exercises since 2021. The Carillon reported in 2026 that JB was last reviewed in April 2021 with no changes; the 2021 minutes call it an “amended” policy. We could not open the 2019 CTV News and Canadian Press stories, the 2019 Carillon editorial, or the Free Press articles listed in the board’s correspondence.
