Research it yourself

Ask your own AI about the board

You don’t have to rely on our reports. Here are two ways to put your own questions about the Hanover School Division board of trustees to an AI assistant, with instructions that keep the answers fair and backed by evidence you can check.

Last updated Nov 14, 2026.

Which option?

  • Option 1: Ask a chat assistant. Quick and easy. Paste a prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. Best for questions our reports already cover.
  • Option 2: Have an AI agent read the official minutes. Takes a little setup and patience. Your AI reads the board’s minutes on hsd.ca itself, meeting by meeting. Best for questions our reports don’t cover.

Option 1: Ask a chat assistant

  1. Open an AI chat assistant that can browse the web (most can; some need web search turned on).
  2. Copy the prompt below, paste it in, and replace the last line with your question.
  3. Open the links to the minutes in the answer for anything that matters to you.
I have a question about the Hanover School Division board of trustees (Manitoba). Please research it using these sources:
- https://hsdreporting.ca/llms.txt (a guide to every report on the site)
- https://hsdreporting.ca/ (the reports themselves)
- https://hsd.ca/governance/agendas-minutes/ (the board's official minutes and agendas, if the reports don't cover my question)

How to answer:
1. Be neutral. Describe what trustees and the division said and did without guessing at motives or taking sides. Where people disagreed, give each side's strongest points in their own words.
2. Back every point with specific evidence: the meeting date, the motion or a short exact quote, and a link to the page of the minutes or the news story where it appears.
3. The minutes record how many trustees voted for and against, not who. Don't say how a trustee voted unless the minutes record a named vote or a news source you cite reports it.
4. Keep three things clearly apart: what the record shows, what the site's analysis says, and your own conclusions.
5. If you can't open these sources, or they don't answer my question, say so plainly. Don't fill gaps from memory or guesswork, and tell me what you could not confirm.
6. Parents, students and other members of the public are not named in these sources. Don't try to identify them.

My question: [type your question here]

Example questions: “How has the board decided which schools hold religious exercises?” · “What did the board decide about the Grade 9 catchments for Kleefeld and Bothwell?” · “How much have school taxes gone up since the provincial freeze ended?”

Option 2: Have an AI agent read the official minutes

The board posts its minutes from July 2013 on, as PDFs, on hsd.ca. Most are scanned paper, so the AI has to read each page as an image or run text recognition on it. An AI agent that can browse and read files (such as Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Gemini CLI, Cursor, or ChatGPT and Claude in agent or research mode) can work through them to answer your question.

  1. Open your AI agent and make sure it can browse the web and read PDFs.
  2. Paste the ground rules below, then your question.
  3. Narrow it down if you can (a year, a school, a policy). Reading every set of minutes takes a while.
You are helping me research the Hanover School Division (Manitoba) board of trustees using its official records. Follow these rules:

1. Start with https://hsdreporting.ca/llms.txt and any report on that site that covers my question. Then read the board's own minutes and agendas at https://hsd.ca/governance/agendas-minutes/ (PDFs from July 2013 on; most are scanned, so read the page images or run text recognition, and check names and numbers on the image).
2. Be neutral. Report what was decided and said. Don't guess at motives, don't take sides, and when people disagreed, give each side's strongest points in their own words.
3. Back every point with the meeting date, the motion number or exact wording, and a link to the PDF page (add #page=N to the PDF address).
4. The minutes give vote counts ("Aye: 7 Nay: 2"), not names. Never work out how a trustee voted from the counts, from who moved or seconded a motion, or from who was absent. Name individual votes only from a recorded vote in the minutes or from a news source you cite.
5. Many matters are decided in closed (in-camera) Committee of the Whole meetings, whose minutes are not published. Not finding something in the public minutes is not proof it never happened.
6. Don't name parents, students or other members of the public who appear in the minutes. Initials at most.
7. Say what you could not find or confirm, and keep quotes and your own summary clearly separate.

My question: [type your question here]

Things to keep in mind

  • AI assistants make mistakes. They can misread a scanned page, mix up two meetings or invent a quote. The links to the minutes are there so you can check, and the division’s minutes are the official record.
  • The board doesn’t post meeting video, so what was said in debate is known only from the brief minutes and from news coverage.
  • Your questions go to your AI provider, not to us. Their privacy terms apply.
