Issue history

Student safety, discipline, phones and school buses

What rules does Hanover School Division follow to keep students safe and to discipline them, who wrote them and when, how has the division handled threats and lockdowns, and what has the board done about phones, school buses and busy crossings since 2013?

Board meetings Jul 2013 – Sept 2026 · 80 decisions in the minutes · Last updated Nov 14, 2026

In short

  • The rulebook was rewritten in stages. The board adopted its first Safe Schools policy in October 2014, rewrote its discipline, substance-use and code of conduct policies in 2020–2021, added a seclusion room policy in April 2022, and replaced the Safe Schools policy with a new Safe and Caring Schools policy in June 2026. Several changes followed new provincial directives.
  • Phones went from “no plans” to a ban. In 2019 the division said it was not planning to ban cellphones. After a pilot at Stonybrook Middle School, trustees voted unanimously in June 2024 to make all K–8 schools cellphone-free, seven weeks before the province banned phone use in every school.
  • Threats and lockdowns reach the board only as news clippings. The minutes list news coverage of more than a dozen threats, lockdowns and weapons incidents from 2018 to 2026, including phoned-in threats that closed every Steinbach school in January 2023. No public board discussion of any of them is recorded; the division’s responses appear in its statements and in the news.
  • Buses and crossings got steady, unanimous attention. HSD runs about 85 bus routes for about 6,000 riders. The board bought bus cameras in 2017 that have led to convictions of drivers who pass stopped buses, and added crossing-guard time at Niverville, Steinbach and Mitchell schools five times.
  • Four split votes, no recorded reasons. Trustees divided twice on the code of conduct in 2022 (7–1, then carried 4–3), on background checks for staff in 2024 (7–1) and on the technology policy in 2025 (8–1). The minutes do not say who voted which way or why.

Background: the rules

Manitoba sets the frame. The province’s Provincial Code of Conduct sets out what every school’s code of conduct must cover, and the Public Schools Act and its Safe Schools Charter, which HSD’s policy cites, set the limits on suspension and expulsion. Over the past decade the province has added policy directives that school boards must write into their own policies: one on seclusion (2021), one on suspension (2023), and rules on cellphones (2024). The board’s job is to adopt division policies that fit those rules; principals and staff apply them day to day.

A board policy becomes official after three “readings”, or votes. HSD usually gives first reading at one meeting and second and third (final) reading at the next, so most policies appear twice in the table below. Day-to-day details sit in administrative procedures, which the superintendent writes and the board does not vote on.

The policies that matter most to families

  • Safe and Caring Schools (AG). Rewritten in June 2026. It commits the division to schools “free from bullying, harassment, discrimination, and identity-based harm,” requires staff to report incidents to the principal, and requires every school to keep an emergency plan with threat assessment protocols, reviewed each year with the school’s Parent Advisory Council.
  • HSD Code of Conduct (AG-A). Lists what parents, staff and students “can expect” and “should” do. Last reviewed in June 2023; read the policy.
  • Student Discipline (JFA). Lists unacceptable behaviour (bullying, threats, weapons, drugs, vaping, misuse of technology) and consequences from a talk with the teacher up to suspension and expulsion. A principal may suspend for up to 5 days and the superintendent for up to 6 weeks; only the board can expel. Since 2024 the policy says HSD “will not use suspension as a response to student presence and engagement” (attendance), and that suspensions will not get longer with each repeat, two rules taken from the province’s 2023 directive. Families can appeal a discipline decision, ending with a 10-minute presentation to the board.
  • Student Substance Use (JFB). Covers alcohol, drugs, tobacco and vapes. A first offence can bring a suspension of one to five days, reduced to three if the student works with the division’s Addictions Foundation of Manitoba (AFM) counsellor.
  • Use of Seclusion Rooms (EG). Seclusion means placing a student alone in a room they cannot leave. The policy allows it only “as a last resort” when a student is an immediate danger to themselves or others.
  • Responsible Use of Technology (AH). Since September 2024, students in kindergarten to Grade 8 may not bring a phone to school, and high school students may not use one in class; read the policy.
  • Background checks (GM). Every new employee and volunteer must have a “Clear” criminal record check and child abuse registry check.
  • Buses (EDC, EDE). Schools close for cold when Environment Canada’s Kleefeld station reads minus 35°C, or a wind chill of minus 45, at 6:00 a.m. On the bus, the driver keeps order and the principal has the same disciplinary authority as at school.

Two terms come up often. In a lockdown, students and staff shelter in place. A Hold and Secure is a lesser step that keeps people outside the building; the board described one at Steinbach Regional Secondary School (SRSS) in 2022 as having “resulted in the locking of entry/exit doors”.

2013–2017: A first safety policy, security staff and bus cameras

School zones and the first crossing guards

In November 2013 the board agreed to write to Steinbach and every municipality with an HSD school asking them to pass reduced speed limits in school zones, and offered the help of its transportation department to keep the zones consistent. By the next spring the RM of Hanover had met division staff about the cost of the school-zone signs. In April 2014 the board approved a crossing guard at 5th Avenue and 3rd Street in Niverville, the first of several crossing-guard decisions in the record.

The Safe Schools policy, 2014

In March 2014 the board received, as information, Education Minister James Allum’s Provincial Code of Conduct on “Appropriate Interventions and Disciplinary Consequences”. HSD’s governance committee then brought Policy AG – Safe Schools to the board, which gave it first reading in May and final reading on Oct 7, 2014, both 9–0. The 2014 text is not online; the companion HSD Code of Conduct dates from June 2014, according to the policy itself.

Through these years the board also received a regular “Suspensions” report as information, such as the July 2013 suspensions report. The figures were not published in the minutes.

Security staff at SRSS, 2015

In December 2015 the board approved hiring external security personnel at the Steinbach Regional Secondary School for three hours over the lunch hour, “to assist school Administration in maintaining a safe and secure environment,” plus up to five hours a day of evening monitoring, 8–0. The minutes do not say what prompted it. An SRSS security guard procedure came back to the governance committee in May 2017.

Buying buses, and choosing where to ride

HSD owns and runs its own fleet. In 2014 and 2017 the board chose not to join the province’s central bus tender and bought on its own: eight diesel buses in 2015 at an average of $92,657.50 each, and seven gas buses in 2017 at an average of $109,800.

For families, a practical change came in June 2015. The board approved “alternate busing”: a student living in a bus-eligible area could be picked up at a second, permanent address, such as a caregiver’s, if it was on an existing route in the same catchment and the bus had room.

Cameras on buses

The 2016/17 budget included money for “bus surveillance cameras, GPS fleet tracking, and a student-passenger tracking system”. In April 2017 the board accepted a tender for bus cameras from GateKeeper, 8–0.

Outside the minutes

Steinbach Online reported that the inside and outside cameras would be phased in over three years at a cost of approximately $60,000 a year. Director of Transportation Rob Warkentin said, “If something happens on a bus, we can look at the tape and see what really happened.” By October 2018, with cameras on 57 of 86 buses, “many motorists” had been convicted of passing stopped school buses. In 2023 Warkentin estimated 70 of the 85 buses had cameras and said passing a stopped bus “is a regular occurrence within our division.”

2018–2019: Threats, lockdowns and telling parents

A threat at Niverville Collegiate, February 2018

The board’s March 2018 minutes list seven news stories about a social media threat against Niverville Collegiate Institute, with headlines such as “Niverville parents outraged following school threat” and “HSD promises change.” The minutes record no discussion.

Outside the minutes

A student had posted a photo of a gun online the night before school. Police located the teen and a BB gun, there was no lockdown and classes went ahead. Parents were upset that they heard about it hours later. According to Steinbach Online, parents were emailed about 10:30 a.m. and the division did not use the automatic phone system it uses for storm closures; Superintendent Randy Dueck said HSD would likely use it next time, and that “We’ve done a lot of learning in the last forty-eight hours.” HSD held a public meeting with parents, RCMP and trustees at the school that week. In November 2018 the Carillon ran a story headlined “HSD Threat response inadequate”, which we could not open.

A run of incidents in 2019

The 2019 minutes list news of a bomb threat that evacuated Clearspring Middle School in January (a hoax, according to the headlines), an online threat against Niverville Collegiate in April, an SRSS lockdown after a reported gunshot in May, and a lockdown of SRSS after an altercation in September, followed by the Carillon headline “HSD to probe on lockdown lapse.” Again, no board discussion is recorded.

Outside the minutes

In April, mySteinbach reported that after the online threat “the school was placed in Hold and Secure” with RCMP notified, the suspect was quickly arrested, and counsellors were available the next day. In September, “The SRSS was placed in immediate lockdown and a call was made to 911” after early reports that a gun had been drawn; RCMP put other Steinbach schools in Hold and Secure until a suspect was arrested off school property. The Carillon headlines that followed, “HSD to probe on lockdown lapse” and “SRSS learns from lockdown incident”, suggest the division reviewed its response; we could not open those stories.

“No serious conversation” about phones

In March 2019, as Ontario moved to restrict phones in class, a Steinbach Online story headlined “HSD not planning to ban cell phones in the classroom” was put before the board’s April meeting. Superintendent Dueck told the station, “We are always talking to kids about responsible use of technology,” and said a ban, if considered, would have to be thought through for early, middle and senior years.

Background checks, bus cancellations and violence at work

In August 2019 the board adopted Policy GM on criminal record and child abuse registry checks, 8–0. According to Steinbach Online, it “will require all employees and volunteers to get” both checks; Superintendent Dueck said it put about 15 years of practice into writing, and volunteers would not be charged for them. The same night the board adopted Policy EDC on cancelling bus service for weather and road conditions.

That fall the board moved video security from board policy to an administrative procedure (rescinding Policy CDA), and in February 2020 adopted Policy EF – Workplace Violence Prevention. EF is about staff: it says the potential for violence exists “given the diversity of the learning needs of students,” and commits the division to assess and reduce that risk, as Manitoba’s workplace safety rules require.

More crossing-guard time

Crossing guards were added or extended at South Oaks and Woodlawn in February 2019 and again at Penner Street and Barkman Avenue for Woodlawn in October 2019. Assistant Superintendent Shelley Amos told Steinbach Online that the route had “only become busier” with buses and parent pickups.

2020–2022: Rewriting the rulebook

New safety, conduct and discipline policies

Between December 2020 and March 2021 the board replaced its student behaviour rules as a set. It amended the Safe Schools policy and the HSD Code of Conduct, retired the old student Code of Behaviour (JF), and adopted revised Student Discipline (JFA) and Student Substance Use (JFB) policies. Every vote was unanimous. The minutes give no summary of what changed. A revised technology policy (then called GA) got first reading in May 2021; no final reading appears in the published minutes.

Also in late 2020, the board updated its service animal policy (EAA) and added an hour a day of crossing guards on Highway 52 in Mitchell, where the elementary and middle schools have different start times. Board chair Ron Falk told Steinbach Online, “We just want to make sure that students are safe and those crossing guards are just an added safety component.”

Bus rules were tidied in the same way: in February 2022 the board re-adopted policies on driver hours of service, bus idling, extracurricular trips and student conduct on the bus. COVID-19 cleaning pay for bus drivers is covered in COVID-19 in Hanover schools.

Seclusion rooms, 2022

In June 2021 Manitoba issued a policy directive on seclusion that said “School divisions are required to revise or develop policies” to match it. Under the directive, seclusion is a safety response, not a punishment; every use “must be reported on the day of the” incident to parents, the student must be watched continuously (“occasional checks are not acceptable”), and divisions must monitor their use of seclusion every year.

HSD’s board adopted its first seclusion policy, EG, on Apr 5, 2022, 8–0, and re-adopted it without recorded debate in April 2025. The policy itself is short. It commits schools to “preventive practices as opposed to those that are punitive and reactive” and allows seclusion only “as a last resort after prevention strategies, de-escalation interventions, and less restrictive measures have been exhausted.” The rules for reporting to parents and for the rooms themselves are in administrative procedures (SS-45 and SS-47) that are not posted with the policy. No HSD figures on how often seclusion is used have been published in the minutes.

Outside the minutes

Before the directive, a University of Manitoba researcher surveyed more than 60 parents whose children with disabilities had been restrained or isolated at school, and found parents were rarely told; she called seclusion “a crisis response”, not a strategy. That report does not name any school division. We found no news report about seclusion rooms in HSD.

The 2022 code of conduct vote

The one sharp division on these policies came in 2022. A further amendment to the HSD Code of Conduct (AG-A) passed first reading 7–1 on May 3 and final reading 4–3 on June 7. The minutes do not say what changed. The Carillon covered it under the headline “HSD board split on code of conduct policy”, which we could not open. The current version, re-adopted 9–0 and 7–0 in June 2023, includes a passage saying the division “respects the role of parents/guardians as the primary educators of their own children, particularly on topics that may be deemed sensitive”; we could not confirm whether that was the 2022 change. The wider debate over parents’ rights and sensitive topics is covered in Gender, sexuality and classroom materials.

Weapons, a police officer for SRSS, and charges against a former teacher

In 2022 the minutes list news of an SRSS student charged after bringing bear spray and a BB gun to school in May, and of two Steinbach schools in Hold and Secure in November after, according to an RCMP headline, a teen was seen carrying a knife. A February 2022 protest at SRSS also led to a Hold and Secure; the board said it was “in full support” of the staff’s decision (see COVID-19 in Hanover schools).

In July 2022, two stories surfaced in the same week, both listed in the news clippings in the board’s Aug 30 minutes. The Carillon reported that “Hanover SD requests greater police presence for regional high school” and that “Steinbach says no to paying for school cop.” RCMP also charged a former SRSS teacher and coach with sexual offences involving students (“HSD responds to serious charges against former teacher,” Steinbach Online). The charges were allegations, not proven in court, and we could not confirm the outcome. The minutes record correspondence from the minister and a “Response Letter from the City of Steinbach” but not their contents, and no public discussion of either matter.

2023–2024: Phoned-in threats, a bus collision and a phone ban

School closures over phoned-in threats, January 2023

The February 2023 minutes list more than a dozen news stories on one episode: a threat that closed Clearspring Middle School, then “More phone threats Thursday, division closes all Steinbach and Kleefeld schools for Friday”, and a U.S. Homeland Security investigation into where the calls came from. Earlier that month Steinbach Online had reported that lockdown drills were planned across the division.

Outside the minutes

The threats were voicemails left at Clearspring Middle School. In a notice reprinted by Steinbach Online, HSD said “We take threats to schools or persons very seriously” and that “In an abundance of caution, ALL STEINBACH SCHOOLS and Kleefeld School will not be open,” with students moving to remote learning for the day. Superintendent Shelley Amos wrote to families that “we received voicemail threats directed to three HSD schools” and that counsellors were available. A Carillon headline listed in the minutes read “Parent applauds HSD protocol amid repeated threats.”

A child struck near the SRSS bus loop, September 2023

On Sept 11, 2023 a six-year-old girl was struck by a school bus near SRSS and airlifted to hospital; the October minutes list CTV, Global, Free Press and Carillon coverage. Steinbach Online confirmed that an “HSD school bus was involved” and that division care teams debriefed students who saw it. A month later Steinbach City Council approved no-parking changes on Chapel Drive at HSD’s request; the station reported that the division had “recently undertook a review of the safety” around SRSS. The girl was home in time for Christmas, according to a headline in the January 2024 minutes. We found no report of charges.

At the same October meeting, the board discussed the “Hands Off Our Kids” march in Steinbach; administration described “the safety planning that took place in particular at the SRSS” and said attendance stayed between 89 and 95 percent. That march is covered in Gender, sexuality and classroom materials.

Background checks amended, February 2024

The board amended Policy GM in early 2024. First reading carried 7–1 on Feb 6; final reading carried 9–0 a month later. The minutes do not say who objected or why. The amended policy makes an offer of work or volunteering contingent on a “Clear” criminal record check and a “Clear” child abuse registry check, lets the division interview an applicant whose check is “Not Clear” to decide whether accommodation is possible, and requires employees to report any change in their status.

Outside the minutes

Province-wide, Manitoba moved in 2023 to create an independent commissioner for teacher misconduct and a “public registry of teachers”, after cases elsewhere in the province.

Threats continue, 2024

An online threat closed SRSS on Mar 1, 2024 and a Steinbach youth was arrested; in April some Steinbach schools went into Hold and Secure, and the Carillon headline read “Youth with BB gun sparks school lockdown.”

The K–8 phone ban, June 2024

In 2023/24 Stonybrook Middle School ran a year-long pilot without cellphones. On Jun 4, 2024 trustees gave unanimous first reading to an amended technology policy, and on June 27 they made it final 8–0: from September 2024, students in kindergarten to Grade 8 may not bring phones to school. High schools were left to set limits on use in class.

“Our suspensions in that school are cut in half from over the same time period last year.”

Assistant Superintendent Leanne Peters, on the Stonybrook pilot, quoted by Steinbach Online, Jun 6, 2024

Superintendent Shelley Amos said keeping phones out of the school day could help “with the social media craze that’s been happening right now.” On Aug 15, 2024 the province announced that every school in Manitoba would ban phone use “during school hours, including lunch and breaks,” for K–8, and during class time for Grades 9–12. HSD’s rules for high schools were aligned in September: K–8 students may not bring phones, and Grades 9–12 may not use them in class, with exceptions for medical and learning needs. The board received a letter from the minister about cell phones as information. In January 2025 Superintendent Joe Thiessen said HSD had planned the change anyway “and the province came alongside of us.”

At the same June 27 meeting the board adopted a revised Student Discipline policy, 7–0 with one abstention, bringing in the province’s 2023 suspension rules. According to Steinbach Online, Trustee Lynn Barkman abstained and Trustee Cheryl Froese asked whether so detailed a policy should be a procedure; Superintendent Amos replied that the wording largely comes from the provincial directive, the station reported. The board also added three hours a day of crossing guards at McKenzie Avenue and Chapel Drive and the Barkman Avenue crosswalk, a common crossing point for Southwood, Stonybrook and SRSS students.

2025–2026: Refreshed policies and new incidents

A round of policy reviews

In early 2025 the board re-adopted the bus cancellation, service animal, technology, seclusion and substance-use policies. Most votes were unanimous; first reading of the technology policy in February carried 8–1, and final reading in March carried 7–0. (An earlier first reading in October 2024 had also passed, 8–0.) The new service animal policy dropped a requirement that animals be certified, which Assistant Superintendent Rod Kehler told Steinbach Online was about accessibility; a doctor or psychologist still has to confirm the need. The policy was revised again in June 2026; that text says a trainer’s letter, including for a self-trained animal, “would be preferred”, but a later section still asks parents for “a certificate of training”.

The minutes show the board receiving a “Suspension Report – Summary” in June 2025, in line with the province’s expectation that divisions track suspensions. The numbers were not published.

On buses, the board dropped three bus policies as board policies in late 2025 (driver hours, idling and extracurricular trips), leaving them to the administration. A final reading of the bus conduct policy (EDE) on Dec 2, 2025 was rescinded in January 2026 and turned into a first reading; the published minutes show no earlier first reading for that revision. The board gave it final reading on Feb 3, 2026. The new text says “New related forms and new procedure coming.”

Incidents in 2025 and 2026

The minutes list news of an SRSS bomb threat in January 2025 (a youth was charged, according to a Carillon headline) and a September 2025 lockdown after a parent allegedly threatened staff. The parent was charged with assault and uttering threats, according to the RCMP; a letter from Blumenort School, quoted by Steinbach Online, said students “were not involved at any point and were kept safe in their classrooms.” On Apr 22, 2026 Green Valley School staff in Grunthal detained a student before police arrived; HSD said “a student had brought a weapon onto school property” and the school was locked down as a precaution. Global News reported RCMP seized an unloaded handgun, ammunition and knives, and that “No force was used by school officials.” The charges in these cases are unproven.

An alleged assault at Stonybrook, February 2026

In February 2026 the mother of an Indigenous Grade 6 student at Stonybrook Middle School said he had been bullied about his race and then assaulted by two classmates; the board’s March minutes list five stories, including “Hanover School Division issues statement following alleged incident.” According to CTV, the two 11-year-olds were suspended, and RCMP said they were too young to be charged. HSD said the incident was under investigation, “including a review of lunch-hour supervision practices,” and that it had started its risk assessment protocol.

“We want to state clearly that violence, racism, and discrimination have no place in our schools.”

Hanover School Division, statement quoted by CTV News, Feb 17, 2026

The minutes record no board discussion of the incident. The same month HSD blocked internet browsing on school Chromebooks for high school students between midnight and 6 a.m.; Assistant Superintendent Marlin Adrian said it was about “helping students arrive at school rested.”

Background checks back in the news

In January 2026 the Winnipeg Free Press published an article titled “Case of the Invisible Teacher” about a former HSD staff member who had worked on limited teaching permits, and the Niverville Citizen followed with “Not on the registry: Charges against former teacher reveal system gaps” (listed in the March minutes). We could not open either article, and any charges are unproven. HSD responded that it had obtained four permits for the person between 2021 and 2024 and that the mandated child abuse registry and criminal record checks were obtained; “At no time did the Division bypass” required safeguards, it said.

The province steps in on school security

The province announced $500,000 for school safety on Dec 1, 2025 and required every division to review its emergency plan, including controlled access to buildings, and “report back to the government by Dec. 25.” Steinbach Online reported that “Hanover School Division endorses province’s enhanced school safety plan”. HSD’s plan has not been published. The June 2026 minutes list a May 31 story on the Manitoba Teachers’ Society calling for action as school violence concerns rise.

A new Safe and Caring Schools policy and a problem-solving policy, June 2026

On Jun 2, 2026, with six trustees present, the board unanimously adopted a rewritten Safe and Caring Schools policy (AG). It goes further than the 2014 policy: it covers “identity-based harm in all forms, including in digital environments,” commits to “restorative, trauma-informed, and progressive approaches,” and requires each school to have a safety advisory committee and to review its emergency plan with parents every year.

The same night it adopted a new Positive Problem-Solving policy (AL). Despite the name, it is not about student discipline: it sets out four steps for a parent with a concern, “Speak with the teacher first,” then the principal, then the superintendent’s office, then an appeal to the board.

HSD now runs about 85 buses on regular routes, carried nearly 6,000 riders in 2025, and said about 110 driving staff get eight hours of training. Seven new buses replaced older ones for fall 2026. Cuts to bus replacement were part of the 2023/24 budget reductions; see Budgets and taxes.

What the new board inherits

The board elected on Oct 28, 2026 (Jenn Elliot, Jeff Friesen, Shayne Barkman, Jacqueline Morgan, Tess Doerksen, Josh Loewen, Jeremy Neufeld, Erin Williams and Cheryl Froese) took office in November 2026. From the record, on this topic:

  • The code of conduct is under review. Policy AG-A was on the governance committee’s agenda in June 2026; no new reading has been recorded. The 2026 Safe and Caring Schools policy says each school will implement it.
  • Bus conduct procedures are pending. The bus conduct policy adopted in February 2026 says new forms and a new procedure are “coming.”
  • Traffic requests are open. A crossing guard request for South Oaks and an SRSS traffic safety and parking review went to the operations committee in April 2026; no board decision on either is recorded.
  • Provincial security plans. Divisions filed updated emergency and controlled-access plans with the province in December 2025; any building changes and their cost are not in the public minutes.
  • Public reporting. The province requires divisions to monitor seclusion yearly and to track suspensions. The board receives suspension summaries, but neither seclusion nor suspension figures have been published in the minutes.

What we searched, and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Searched: all 1,660 motions; the full text of every set of minutes and agenda for safe, bully, threat, lockdown, hold and secure, violence, weapon, police, RCMP, seclusion, restraint, suspension, discipline, conduct, cell, phone, technology, vape, drug, substance, security, camera, vulnerable sector, service animal, bus, crossing guard and school zone; the current texts of policies AG, AG-A, AL, AH, EAA, ED, EDC, EDE, EF, EG, GM, JFA and JFB; HSD news posts; and Steinbach Online, mySteinbach, Golden West, Global, CTV/CP24, Niverville Online, provincial news releases and Manitoba Education directives.
  • The record’s limits: HSD publishes no meeting video. Minutes record vote counts, not who voted which way, and rarely summarize discussion or what a policy amendment changed. Discipline appeals, expulsions and personnel matters go to closed meetings. Threats and incidents appear in the minutes only as lists of news headlines; where we could not open a story, we report only its headline as the minutes list it. Older versions of policies are not online, so we could not compare texts.
  • Couldn’t confirm: what the 2022 code of conduct amendment changed and why three trustees opposed it; who opposed the 2024 background-check amendment and the 2025 technology policy, and why; why SRSS security staff were hired in 2015; what HSD asked of the RCMP and the City of Steinbach in 2022 and what the city’s letter said; the outcome of the 2022 charges against a former SRSS teacher and the details of the 2026 limited-permit case; whether a final reading of the 2021 technology policy (GA) was ever recorded; how often seclusion rooms are used, how many suspensions there are, or whether HSD reports either publicly; whether anyone was charged after the September 2023 bus collision; and what is in HSD’s December 2025 safety plan. We could not open Carillon, CBC or Winnipeg Free Press articles.
  • Vote note: a Steinbach Online story on the Jun 27, 2024 meeting reported a 7–1 vote with Trustee Cheryl Froese opposed. According to that story, the 7–1 vote was on the communicable diseases policy; the cellphone policy passed 8–0.
