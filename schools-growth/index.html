Issue history

Growing enrolment: new schools, crowded classrooms and catchment changes

Hanover School Division keeps adding students. How has the board tried to make room for them since 2013, what did it ask the province for and get, and what did each new school, portable classroom and catchment change mean for families?

Board meetings Jul 2013 – Sept 2026 · 53 decisions in the minutes · Last updated Nov 14, 2026

In short

  • About 1,400 more students. The province counted 7,721 students in Hanover schools in September 2013 and 9,156 in October 2025, an increase of about 19%, while public-school enrolment in Manitoba as a whole grew about 9%. In 2025 Hanover had the largest increase of any division.
  • The province decides what gets built. The board ranks its needs in a five-year capital plan; the province chooses and pays. The results: an expanded Steinbach Regional Secondary School (2014), Niverville High School (2019), a Mitchell Elementary addition (2020), a Mitchell Middle addition, Parkhill School in Steinbach (2025), and a Green Valley School addition in Grunthal that the province put on hold in 2024 and revived in 2025.
  • Stopgaps in between. Portable (modular) classrooms at Kleefeld, Niverville, Mitchell and South Oaks; small additions at Blumenort, South Oaks and Niverville Elementary; and grade moves, such as keeping Grade 5 at South Oaks in 2025 to free space at Green Valley.
  • Catchment changes moved children between schools. Steinbach’s early-years boundaries were redrawn for Parkhill in October 2024 and again in December 2024. From fall 2026, Grade 9 students from Kleefeld go to Green Valley and from Bothwell to Niverville High, because Steinbach Regional Secondary is projected to pass 2,300 students. Several of these decisions appear in no published vote.
  • One split vote. Every recorded vote on this topic was unanimous except a June 2018 request to move a property from the Niverville to the Landmark catchment, defeated 3–4.
  • Still short of space. In 2025 the division asked the province for a new high school. We found no provincial commitment to build it as of November 2026.

Background: the rules

Who pays for new schools

School boards in Manitoba do not decide on their own to build a school. Each year the board sends the province a five-year capital plan: a ranked list of the new schools, additions, portable classrooms and major repairs it says it needs. The province picks which projects to fund. Until the early 2020s that work went through the Public Schools Finance Board (PSFB), the provincial agency “with responsibility for financing major capital projects”; by 2023 the minutes refer to the province’s Capital Project Planning and Delivery (CPPD) office instead. When the province told Hanover in 2024 that a project was on hold, it asked the division to confirm the project’s priority “within the school division’s future 5-year capital plan submissions”.

Once a project is approved, the board signs the borrowing paperwork as the bills come in: debenture or borrowing by-laws until 2020, and provincial promissory notes from 2021. Those approvals make up most of the decisions on this page, and they show which projects were moving. How that borrowing is paid for, and what it means for school taxes, is covered in Budget and taxes.

Catchments

A catchment is the area each school serves. The division says it “maintains strict catchment areas in order to ensure a balanced enrollment across the division and the viability of all schools”. Families can apply to attend another school through Schools of Choice, but space is limited and, as the superintendent wrote in 2024, “HSD does not provide bus transportation for students attending schools outside their designated catchment area”. So when the board moves a catchment line or moves a grade to another building, children change schools and bus routes change with them.

A note on “school closure”

The board passed its Policy CA, titled “School Closure”, in 2015 and revised it in 2019, 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2026. Despite the name, the current policy is about closing schools for a day because of storms, bad roads or extreme cold, and how staff are paid on those days, not about closing a school for good. Those readings are not counted on this page. We found no decision in the minutes to close a school permanently; this page is about the opposite problem.

How many students

The province publishes an enrolment count for every division each fall. Hanover’s count fell only twice between 2013 and 2025: by 16 students in 2014, and by 593 in 2020, when enrolment fell across Manitoba during the COVID-19 pandemic and home-schooling rose sharply (see COVID-19).

2013–2016: A bigger SRSS, portables and small additions

The SRSS addition is finished

When the minutes begin in mid-2013, the largest project under way was an addition and renovation at Steinbach Regional Secondary School (SRSS), the division’s high school for Steinbach and the surrounding villages. Between September 2013 and November 2014 the board gave first reading to seven borrowing by-laws that included money for “SRSS – New School Addition / Renovation”, such as $2,630,600 in September 2013 and $4,582,300 in September 2014. A trustee committee, the Steinbach High School Committee, oversaw the work. The expansion’s grand opening was set for Sept 23, 2014, and in February 2015 the board dissolved the committee 8–0 because it had finished “all tasks assigned in the establishment of the Steinbach Regional Secondary Addition / Expansion”. Two of the by-laws also carried money for “CMS – New School”, Clearspring Middle School in Steinbach.

In December 2014 the board also decided to move Grade 9 from the Bothwell, Blumenort, Kleefeld and Mitchell schools to SRSS from 2015/16, a program decision covered in Learning and programs. Eleven years later, crowding at SRSS would send some of those Grade 9 students elsewhere (see 2025–2026).

Portables from the province

Short of an addition, the quickest way to add space was a modular (portable) classroom, also supplied through the PSFB. In May 2014 the board approved borrowing of $1,150,200 for modular classrooms at Niverville Collegiate. That summer it wrote to the PSFB about portables at SRSS, and the PSFB agreed to provide one modular classroom at Kleefeld School for fall 2014. In February 2017 the board was told approval had been granted for three more modular classrooms at Niverville Collegiate for 2017/18.

Hanover also does work for the province here. By 2014 its facilities staff were already managing the province’s modular classroom program, monitoring installations, scheduling existing units and storing the inventory. The board agreed to continue “as in past years” in 2014, with a suggested fee of 1.85% plus $5,000 for storage, set a bonus in 2016 for the Director of Facilities and Secretary-Treasurer “for extra work completed on behalf of the Province of Manitoba”, paid from those fees, and renewed the arrangement with the province’s new capital office in May 2025.

Small additions in Blumenort, South Oaks, Niverville and Mitchell

Several schools got two to five classrooms. In August 2015 the board accepted the lowest tender for a two-classroom addition at Blumenort School, $1,282,596 from Con Pro Industries, subject to PSFB approval, and received the tender report for a four-classroom addition at South Oaks School in Grunthal. Grand openings for both additions were set for Sept 6, 2016. Drawings were under way for a Niverville Elementary addition, paid for through 2016–2018 as a “Niverville Elem Five Classroom” project, and the PSFB approved a four-classroom addition at Mitchell Elementary, later changed to add washrooms for kindergarten classes.

One rural property changes catchment

In April 2015 the board moved a property at the corner of Highway 52 and PR 216 from the Kleefeld to the Bothwell catchment, 8–0, “to formalize a grandfathered arrangement on that property”. In other words, the family’s children were already going to Bothwell; the vote made it official.

2016–2019: Niverville gets a high school

From portables to a provincial priority

Niverville was growing fast, and Niverville Collegiate, the town’s school for the middle and high-school grades, was relying on portables. As early as November 2013 the board sent the Town of Niverville a letter of intent about a “Potential Future Niverville School Site”, subject to PSFB approval. In June 2016 the board’s information package included a Niverville Citizen article titled “Hanover Requests New 650-Seat Elementary School for Niverville”; what the province eventually funded was a high school.

In May 2017 the province said new schools for Winkler and Niverville were “our top priority projects based on high enrolment in these divisions”. On Jun 16, 2017 Education Minister Ian Wishart announced “a new high school for 450 grade 9 to 12 students in Niverville”, expandable to 550, with a child-care centre.

“This request has been the top item in our five year plan to the PSFB (Public Schools Finance Board) for a number of years.”

Ron Falk, HSD board chair · HSD news release, Jun 16, 2017

Site, tender and sod-turning

In July 2017 the division announced an agreement in principle to buy serviced land from the Town of Niverville on the west side of town, next to the town’s planned recreation centre. A trustee Niverville Building Committee met through 2017 and 2018; the board approved per-meeting pay for trustees on it, 7–0. At the town’s request the province split construction into two phases, leaving the band room, drama room and child-care centre for completion by spring 2020.

“A new high school for Niverville is long overdue… Niverville is one of the fastest growing communities in Manitoba”

Shannon Martin, MLA for Morris, on behalf of the education minister · HSD news release, Jan 19, 2018

Red Lake Construction won the contract at a base cost of $18,450,000, and the sod was turned on Mar 23, 2018. The division named Landmark Collegiate vice-principal Kimberley Funk as the first principal. From March 2018 to December 2020 the board approved borrowing by-laws for the “New Niverville High School” several times a year, beginning with $2,009,900 in March 2018; a final provincial note of $996,600 followed in June 2023.

New names, and what it meant for students

In October 2018 the board announced the new school would be called Niverville High School, after a naming committee received over 100 name suggestions. The board’s December package included a Carillon editorial titled “Niverville school name choice disappointing”. No vote on the name appears in the published minutes.

For families the bigger change was the grade split. When the high school opened in September 2019, Niverville Collegiate became Niverville Middle School for Grades 5–8, so Grade 9–12 students moved to the new building and the old one was renovated for younger students. The province’s 2019 enrolment report lists one new Hanover school opened and Niverville Collegiate renamed.

2018–2021: Mitchell, land in west Steinbach, and boundary fixes

Mitchell Elementary gets five more classrooms

Mitchell Elementary had already had a portable added ($306,500 for one modular classroom in 2018). In late 2018 the board’s package carried a news report titled “Mitchell statistics prove need for elementary school expansion”. The sod was turned on Dec 19, 2019 for an addition with five classrooms, a library and a multipurpose room, at an estimated $4.5 million.

“Enrollment has been steadily increasing at Mitchell Elementary School and is expected to reach upwards of 650 students by the year 2028.”

Hanover School Division, news release · Dec 19, 2019

The board approved $4,000,000 of borrowing for the “Mitchell Elem Addition” in March 2020. A Steinbach Online report in the board’s January 2021 package was titled “Mitchell Elementary School Moves Into Long-Awaited Addition” (Dec 7, 2020).

Land for a school in west Steinbach

The board was also preparing for a new school in Steinbach before the province had promised one. A Steinbach Online article titled “New School Site Chosen in Steinbach” (Dec 22, 2016) was in the January 2017 package. In October 2017 the board accepted, 8–0, the minutes of a closed e-mail meeting labelled “Parkhill”, held Aug 23, 2017; its content was not published. In March 2020 a borrowing by-law included $1,066,000 for “West Steinbach Land”. Parkhill School was later built on Parkland Drive in west Steinbach; the minutes do not describe the land purchase itself.

A catchment request fails, and rural boundaries are tidied up

On Jun 28, 2018 the board voted on a request to move a property “from Niverville to Landmark effective the 2018/19 school year”; it was defeated 3–4. The minutes do not identify the property or record the discussion (see Where trustees disagreed).

In October 2020 a family living near New Bothwell presented a request to the board to change the school their address was assigned to. In November the board granted it for the rest of 2020/21, 9–0, and promised to review all division boundaries for “geographical exceptions such as creeks and seasonal roads, while taking road access into account”. That review led in March 2021 to catchment changes for several rural road segments “based on accessibility to maintained roadways”, moving them to the New Bothwell and Grunthal catchments, 8–0. The stated reason was access over maintained roads, which is what school buses use.

Outside the minutes

Enrolment kept climbing until the pandemic. HSD expected 2.5% more students in 2020/21; instead the provincial count fell by 593, the third-largest drop of any division, as families across Manitoba turned to home-schooling. By the fall of 2022 Hanover was 628 students (8.1%) above the year before, past its pre-pandemic level.

2021–2025: Three provincial promises, one put on hold

April 2021: a K–4 school, a Mitchell Middle addition and a Green Valley gym

In its 2021 budget the province said it would “expedite the province’s commitment to build 20 news [new] schools”. Three Hanover projects were on its list: a new kindergarten to Grade 4 school in west Steinbach (design phase), and a gym addition and renovation at Green Valley School and a four-classroom addition at Mitchell Middle School. The division called them new capital projects for HSD.

Meanwhile the board approved a provincial note of $8,373,500 for “Modular Classrooms” in March 2021, at the same meeting where it received a “Modular Classroom Bulk Build Support Letter”. The minutes do not say which schools those units were for, and Hanover also runs the provincial modular program described above.

Parkhill School: from design to tender

A trustee building committee for the new school, first called the Steinbach West Building Committee, reported from February 2022. In June 2023 the board accepted Penn-Co Construction’s tender of $39,740,119, 7–0, subject to the province’s approval. The ground was broken on Jul 26, 2023, with a school “with an initial capacity for 500 students and an ability to expand by 100 students in the future”. Trustee Danielle Funk is identified in the division’s photo caption as Building Committee Chair. In June 2024 the board announced the name Parkhill School, and in October 2024 it appointed Kim Koop, principal of Landmark Elementary, as its first principal.

Mitchell Middle’s four classrooms

Construction on the Mitchell Middle School addition began in June 2023: about 5,400 square feet, $3.6 million, four classrooms and washrooms, scheduled to open in September 2024. The province said it would accommodate 100 more students in a school that then had about 290 students. The board approved provincial notes for it in June 2023 ($1,946,200) and May 2025 ($1,434,700).

SRSS nears 2,000 students

By 2023 the high school that had been expanded in 2014 was filling again. Superintendent Shelley Amos told Steinbach Online that the division was asking for portables at Niverville Middle, Stonybrook Middle and SRSS, “which is our highest priority because of the burgeoning amount of enrolment that’s happening at that school”:

“We may tip over the 2,000-student mark next fall.”

Shelley Amos, superintendent, to Steinbach Online · May 8, 2023

The same report said SRSS had about 1,400 students before its addition and over 1,900 in 2023.

Green Valley: the addition is deferred

Green Valley School in Grunthal usually served Grades 5 to 12. To make way for the gym addition promised in 2021, its portable classrooms were removed. Then, on Aug 28, 2024, the province told the division the project “has been deferred”.

“we would like to express our sincere disappointment with this decision. While we understand the fiscal challenges the government has encountered, the identified needs that this project was to address still persist.”

Hanover School Division, statement on the deferred Green Valley project · Aug 28, 2024

At its meeting the day before, the board had decided to invite the Minister of Education to Grunthal “to view firsthand the loss of space with the removal of portable classrooms in preparation for construction”, ask to meet the province’s capital office, and have administration look at other ways to find classroom space.

The answer came from a neighbouring school. On Feb 4, 2025 the board voted 9–0 “to move Grade 5 programming from Green Valley School to South Oaks School beginning the 2025/26 school year”. The division said the change addressed “significant capacity challenges at Green Valley” and thanked parents for their input during consultation. For families, it meant about 65 Grade 4 students stayed at South Oaks for Grade 5 instead of moving up.

“We’ll just say that they’ve done an amazing job using all the space available, but this should help out Green Valley next year… The school is still going to be full, just a little less crowded.”

Joe Thiessen, superintendent, to Steinbach Online · Feb 6, 2025

Steinbach Online reported that the move would let Green Valley get back spaces it had converted into classrooms, “such as their library”. In April 2025 the provincial budget put the Green Valley project back. “This was something that was on the table, and then removed, and now back on the table to happen,” the superintendent told Steinbach Online.

Steinbach’s catchments are redrawn, twice

A new school means new catchment lines for every early-years school in Steinbach. On Oct 11, 2024 the superintendent wrote to parents that the board “has approved new catchment areas” for 2025/26, designed “to balance enrollment, minimize the need for children to cross major intersections, and prioritize maintaining student friendships” as they move to middle school. Parkhill and Southwood students would go on to Stonybrook Middle; Elmdale and Woodlawn students to Clearspring Middle. Students already placed through Schools of Choice could stay, but approval of new requests within the division was “expected to be very limited”. No vote on these catchments appears in the published minutes.

“Elmdale, Woodlawn, and Southwood are all at capacity… this is going to allow them to kind of spread out a little bit”

Joe Thiessen, superintendent, to Steinbach Online · Oct 11, 2024

Seven weeks later the map changed again. With only five of nine trustees present, the board voted 5–0 on Dec 3, 2024 to approve “the adjusted Steinbach school catchments beginning the 2025/26 school year”. The superintendent told Steinbach Online that 61 new students had moved into the Parkhill area since work on the map began and that the school “would be full basically, when we opened it”. Children north of Woodhaven Avenue and west of Brandt Street were reassigned from Parkhill to Southwood, and a small group from Elmdale to Southwood; their parents were notified by e-mail the next day.

2025–2026: Parkhill opens and SRSS reaches its limit

Parkhill opens, not quite finished

Parkhill School opened for students on Sept 4, 2025, with room for 500 children in kindergarten to Grade 4 and a 104-space child-care centre. According to CBC, it was the first new school in Steinbach since Clearspring Middle School, built in 2011. The province listed it among three new Manitoba schools that fall.

Outside the minutes

CBC News reported in September 2025 that Parkhill opened while brickwork and play areas were still being finished, that it had 331 students, with room for 500, and that SRSS, with about 2,100 students, was “approaching its capacity of 2,200”. “Many of our earlier schools were busting at the seams, maybe all of them,” Superintendent Joe Thiessen told CBC. CBC reported that a parent, A.P., appreciated her daughter’s less crowded classroom but “did feel the opening was a little rushed”. According to CBC, the division’s next priority after Green Valley is more space for younger grades in Niverville.

Green Valley breaks ground

The Green Valley addition, now estimated at $14.6 million, broke ground on Nov 18, 2025. It adds a gym, a fitness room and three classrooms, turns the old gym into a performing-arts space and adds two science rooms, with full completion expected by November 2027. In September 2026 the board approved a provincial note that included two modular classroom units for South Oaks ($406,100), the school that took Green Valley’s Grade 5 classes.

A request for a new high school

In May 2025 the division sent the province an updated five-year capital plan that, Steinbach Online reported, “this time… includes a request for a new school”. “The requested school would be a high school,” for about 600 students, according to the same report, alongside renovations, modular additions and four-classroom additions at growing schools. The board received the next plan, for 2027–2032, in May 2026 as “the final Five Year Capital Plan sent to the province”. The plans themselves are not published with the minutes.

December 2025: Grade 9 students are sent away from SRSS

On Dec 2, 2025 the division announced “a series of coordinated measures” to relieve SRSS, which it said was “at or near capacity” with “limited course availability and concerns about overcrowding, building congestion, and traffic”. Starting in 2026/27:

  • Grade 9 students from the Kleefeld catchment attend Green Valley School in Grunthal, and Grade 9 students from the Bothwell catchment attend Niverville High School.
  • Green Valley becomes a Grades 7–12 school and South Oaks a K–6 school; students in Grade 5 at South Oaks stay there for Grade 6.
  • Blumenort students may ask to attend Landmark Collegiate instead of SRSS, “subject to enrollment capacity and overall interest”, since a minimum number is needed to run programs and buses.
  • Students already in high school can stay where they are until they graduate, and Grade 10 students can still apply to SRSS’s vocational programs.

For Kleefeld and Bothwell families this reverses part of the 2015 change that moved their Grade 9 students to SRSS (see Learning and programs). The division said “transition planning, including busing and school-level support” would be shared in the months ahead.

The release says the board “has approved” these measures and that they “were announced at the Board of Trustees Public Meeting” that evening. The published minutes of that meeting record no motion and no announcement on catchments; the board had held closed meetings on Nov 4, Nov 18 and Nov 21, 2025, whose minutes are not published. Two months later the board’s package included a Steinbach Online article titled “Kleefeld parent raises concern over Hanover School Division catchment changes” (Feb 11, 2026); we could not open that article.

Where trustees disagreed

The minutes record how many trustees voted for and against, not who, except when a trustee asks for a recorded vote. On this topic only one vote was split:

Every other recorded vote was unanimous. Several major choices, including the October 2024 Parkhill catchments, the names of Niverville High School and Parkhill School, and the December 2025 Grade 9 changes, have no public vote at all, so whether trustees disagreed about them cannot be known from the record. The December 2024 catchment adjustment passed 5–0 with four trustees absent. On how closed meetings work, see Board governance.

What the new board inherits

The board elected on Oct 28, 2026 (Jenn Elliot, Jeff Friesen, Shayne Barkman, Jacqueline Morgan, Tess Doerksen, Josh Loewen, Jeremy Neufeld, Erin Williams and Cheryl Froese) took office in November 2026. On this topic the record leaves it with:

  • More students. The 2026/27 budget was built on 298.5 more FTE students than the year before, and about 20 extra teachers to teach them.
  • The first year of the Grade 9 changes. Kleefeld Grade 9 students at Green Valley, Bothwell Grade 9 students at Niverville High, Grade 6 at South Oaks, and an optional Blumenort-to-Landmark transfer were all set to begin in fall 2026. The division’s catchment page still named SRSS as the high school for Kleefeld and New Bothwell students in November 2026.
  • Construction at Green Valley, due to finish by November 2027, with Green Valley gym updates on the board’s agenda each month since February 2026.
  • A request for a high school. The May 2025 and May 2026 capital plans went to the province; we found no provincial announcement of a new Hanover high school as of November 2026.
  • Space for younger grades. The superintendent told CBC that younger grades in Niverville are next on the list. Parkhill’s first principal said in June 2025 that it would open “with some rooms that are still empty”, after its catchment had already been shrunk once because of growth nearby.

About this page

Built from the board’s published minutes (Jul 2013 – Nov 2026), agendas, policies and news releases on hsd.ca, the province’s annual enrolment reports and news releases, and news coverage linked in the text. See How these reports are made and Data accuracy.

What we searched, and what we couldn’t confirm
  • Searched: all 1,660 motions and the full text of every set of minutes for capital plans, the PSFB and the province’s capital office, modular and portable classrooms, additions, new schools, land, building committees, catchments and boundaries, grade changes, enrolment and capacity, plus the lists of news articles the board received at each meeting; HSD news releases and capital-project pages; the province’s enrolment reports for 2013–2025 and its 2017 and 2021 school-building releases; Steinbach Online, MySteinbach, CBC and Global News.
  • Enrolment figures are the province’s fall counts for the division (the count date moved from Sept 30 to Oct 1 in 2025). The percentages in the summary compare September 2013 with October 2025: Hanover 7,721 to 9,156 (+18.6%); all Manitoba public schools 181,457 to 198,589 (+9.4%). The division’s own FTE figures differ and are labelled where used.
  • The record’s limits: HSD publishes no meeting video. The minutes record vote counts, not how each trustee voted, and list most facilities items by title only. Several decisions on sites, names and catchments appear in no published vote; the board also meets in closed (in-camera) Committee of the Whole meetings, whose minutes are adopted in public but not published. The five-year capital plans are not published with the minutes.
  • Couldn’t confirm: when and where the board decided the October 2024 Parkhill catchments, the two school names and the December 2025 Grade 9 changes; which property the June 2018 catchment request concerned and why it failed; whether the 2021 “Modular Classrooms” note and the 2026 “Provincial Modular Classroom Units” notes paid for portables at Hanover schools or for the provincial program Hanover administers; when the Mitchell Middle and Niverville Elementary additions opened; what the 2015 version of Policy CA covered; busing arrangements and the number of Blumenort transfers for 2026/27; whether the province has answered the request for a new high school. Several news articles listed in the board’s packages (for example on the Kleefeld parent’s concern, SRSS capacity in 2023, and Green Valley in 2024) could not be opened, and are cited only by their titles in the minutes.
